## Understanding the Basics of Cetacean Communication

Cetaceans, including whales, dolphins, and porpoises, are highly social creatures that rely heavily on sound for communication, navigation, and foraging. Their underwater world is one of intricate acoustic signals, ranging from the haunting songs of humpback whales to the rapid clicks of dolphins used for echolocation. Understanding these signals is crucial for comprehending their behavior and protecting them in an increasingly noisy ocean.

*   **Whale Songs:** Complex sequences of sounds, often used for mating displays and long-distance communication. These songs can travel hundreds, even thousands, of kilometers.
*   **Dolphin Clicks:** Short, broadband pulses used for echolocation, allowing dolphins to "see" their surroundings by interpreting the echoes.
*   **Whistles:** Frequency-modulated sounds used for communication within dolphin pods, including individual identification and social signaling.
*   **Pulsed Calls:** Bursts of sound used by various cetacean species for communication, often associated with specific behaviors or social interactions.
