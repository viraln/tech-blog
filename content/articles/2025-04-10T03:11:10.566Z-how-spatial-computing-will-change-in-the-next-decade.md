## What is Spatial Computing?

Spatial computing is the next evolution of human-computer interaction.  It transcends the limitations of screens and keyboards, creating immersive digital experiences that understand and respond to our presence and actions within a three-dimensional space. Unlike traditional computing, which relies on flat interfaces, spatial computing uses sensors, cameras, and AI to map and interact with the real world, overlaying digital information onto our physical surroundings. ## Spatial Computing vs. Augmented Reality: Understanding the Nuances

While often used interchangeably, spatial computing is a broader concept than augmented reality. AR is a *subset* of spatial computing.  AR overlays digital information onto the real world, while spatial computing encompasses a wider range of technologies that interact with and understand the 3D environment.  Consider this table:

| Feature         | Spatial Computing                               | Augmented Reality                               |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Scope           | Broader, encompassing AR, VR, MR, etc.          | Narrower, focusing on overlaying digital content |
| Interaction     | More complex and nuanced interactions possible   | Primarily visual overlays                          |
| Environmental Awareness | Deep understanding and interaction with the environment | Limited to visual overlay on the environment     |
