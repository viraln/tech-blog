Holographic telepresence utilizes a combination of advanced technologies to create the illusion of a person being present in a remote location.  The process typically involves:

1. **High-resolution 3D capture:** Specialized cameras capture a detailed three-dimensional image of a person, often using multiple viewpoints and advanced depth-sensing technologies.  ![High-resolution 3D camera setup](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1603847734787-9e8a3f3e9d60?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
*High-resolution 3D camera setup*

2. **Data compression and transmission:** The massive amount of data generated from the 3D capture is compressed and transmitted over a high-bandwidth network, such as 5G or dedicated fiber optic lines.  This requires significant processing power and efficient compression algorithms.

3. **Holographic reconstruction:**  At the receiving end, a holographic display system reconstructs the 3D image, creating a lifelike projection of the person.  This might involve advanced laser projection techniques or other display methods. ![Example of a holographic display reconstructing a person](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1545431781-3e1b506e9a37?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

4. **Real-time interaction:**  Ideally, the system allows for real-time interaction, enabling participants to engage in natural conversation and gestures.  This requires low latency communication to prevent delays that disrupt the experience.
