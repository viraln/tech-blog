Several key technologies are driving the advancements in spacetime sensing technology. These include:

*   **LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging):** LiDAR systems emit laser pulses and measure the time it takes for the light to return, providing highly accurate distance measurements. Advanced LiDAR systems can capture data at high speeds, enabling real-time 4D sensing.
*   **Radar (Radio Detection and Ranging):** Radar uses radio waves to detect objects and measure their distance and velocity. Radar is particularly useful in challenging environmental conditions, such as fog or rain, where LiDAR may be less effective.
*   **Cameras (RGB and Depth):** Standard RGB cameras capture color images, while depth cameras provide information about the distance to objects. Combining RGB and depth data allows for a more complete understanding of the environment.
*   **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs):** IMUs measure acceleration and angular velocity, providing information about the motion of a sensor or object. IMUs are often used in conjunction with other sensors to improve accuracy and robustness.
*   **Time-of-Flight (ToF) Sensors:** ToF sensors measure the time it takes for light to travel from the sensor to an object and back, providing distance information. ToF sensors are often used in mobile devices and other consumer electronics.

These technologies, often used in combination, enable the creation of detailed and dynamic spacetime maps. The selection of the appropriate technology depends on the specific application and environmental conditions.
