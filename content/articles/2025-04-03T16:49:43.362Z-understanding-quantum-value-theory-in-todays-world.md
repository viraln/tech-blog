## Key Quantum Concepts Applied to Finance

Several core quantum physics concepts provide a framework for understanding QVT:

*   **Superposition:** In quantum mechanics, a particle can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. In finance, this could be analogous to a stock having multiple potential valuations based on different interpretations of available information, investor sentiment, and future prospects. Only when the "measurement" (investment decision) is made does one valuation "collapse" into reality.

*   **Entanglement:** When two quantum particles are entangled, they are linked in such a way that the state of one instantly influences the state of the other, regardless of distance. In finance, this could represent how seemingly unrelated assets or markets can be deeply interconnected and influence each other's behavior, creating unexpected correlations and ripple effects.

*   **Observer Effect:** The act of observing a quantum system changes its state. In finance, this is akin to how investor sentiment and the act of analyzing a stock can influence its price, either positively or negatively, irrespective of its intrinsic value. The more attention a stock receives, the more volatile it might become.

*   **Quantum Tunneling:** A particle can pass through a barrier even if it doesn't have enough energy to overcome it classically. In finance, this might represent unexpected market breakthroughs or sudden shifts in investor confidence that defy conventional analysis. Think of a small company suddenly becoming a market leader, seemingly overnight.
