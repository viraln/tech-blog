## What are Non-Euclidean Access Points?

At its core, a Non-Euclidean access point leverages the principles of non-Euclidean geometry – primarily hyperbolic or elliptic geometry – to create a network topology that differs drastically from traditional, flat (Euclidean) networks. Non-Euclidean geometries break these rules.

*   **Hyperbolic Geometry:** Imagine a saddle shape. In hyperbolic space, parallel lines diverge, and the angles of a triangle add up to *less* than 180 degrees. This allows for exponential scaling of area, which is crucial for efficiently covering vast networks.

*   **Elliptic Geometry:** Envision the surface of a sphere. Parallel lines eventually intersect, and the angles of a triangle add up to *more* than 180 degrees. This geometry can be useful for creating highly connected, localized networks.

Non-Euclidean access points strategically position themselves within these curved spaces (mathematically, of course, not physically bending the laws of nature!), allowing for network architectures that offer significant advantages over traditional linear or grid-based systems.
