These devices interface directly with the nervous system, allowing researchers to bypass damaged areas and restore lost function. Beyond neuroprosthetics, cyborg implants are being used to treat a variety of other medical conditions. Pacemakers regulate heart rhythm, insulin pumps deliver precise doses of insulin to diabetics, and deep brain stimulators alleviate the symptoms of Parkinson's disease. These devices are not just treating symptoms; they are actively restoring the body's ability to function properly.

Furthermore, research into bioartificial organs is gaining momentum. Scientists are working to create artificial kidneys, livers, and even hearts that can be implanted into patients with organ failure. While these technologies are still in the early stages of development, they hold the potential to eliminate the need for organ donation and save countless lives.
