## What is Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI)?

Self-sovereign identity, at its core, is a decentralized approach to digital identity. It empowers individuals to create and control their own digital identity without relying on intermediaries. This is achieved through the use of blockchain technology, decentralized identifiers (DIDs), and verifiable credentials.

🔑 **Key Concepts:**

*   **Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs):** Unique identifiers that are not controlled by any central authority. They are cryptographically linked to the individual's digital identity.
*   **Verifiable Credentials (VCs):** Digital representations of real-world credentials, such as diplomas, licenses, or certificates. These credentials are issued by trusted organizations and can be verified cryptographically.
*   **Digital Identity Wallet:** A secure application that allows individuals to store and manage their DIDs and VCs.

SSI flips the traditional model on its head. Instead of relying on centralized providers to verify your identity, you present verifiable credentials directly to the requesting party. This minimizes the amount of personal information you share and gives you complete control over your data.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Think of SSI as the digital equivalent of a physical wallet, where you store your ID cards, licenses, and other important documents. You only show the information needed for a specific transaction, without revealing your entire identity.

## SSI Technology: The Building Blocks

SSI relies on a combination of technologies working together to create a secure and decentralized identity ecosystem.

*   **Blockchain:** Provides a tamper-proof ledger for recording and verifying identity information. While not always strictly necessary, the immutability of a blockchain offers a significant advantage.

*   **Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs):** Unique, globally resolvable identifiers that are not controlled by any central authority. DIDs are the foundation of SSI.

*   **Verifiable Credentials (VCs):** Digital representations of real-world credentials, issued by trusted organizations and cryptographically signed. These are the "proofs" you present.

*   **Digital Identity Wallets:** Secure applications that allow individuals to store and manage their DIDs and VCs. These act as your digital "passport" and document holder.

*   **W3C Standards:** The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) plays a crucial role in standardizing SSI technologies, ensuring interoperability and adoption.
