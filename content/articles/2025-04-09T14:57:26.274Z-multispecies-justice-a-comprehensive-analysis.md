## What is Multispecies Justice?

Multispecies justice, at its core, is a theoretical and practical framework that seeks to address the ethical and ecological imbalances inherent in human-dominated societies. It moves beyond the traditional human-centric focus of justice systems, extending moral consideration and legal standing to non-human beings and the environment itself.  This means recognizing the inherent worth and rights of all living beings, acknowledging the interconnectedness of life, and working towards a more equitable distribution of resources and opportunities across species. ## Multispecies Justice Definition and Theory: Key Concepts

The definition of multispecies justice is constantly evolving, but several key concepts underpin the movement:

* **Relationality:**  This emphasizes the interconnectedness of all living beings and the importance of understanding our relationships within the larger ecological web.
* **Interspecies Solidarity:**  This involves actively working to dismantle systems of oppression that harm non-human species and striving for mutual flourishing.
* **Environmental Justice:** This crucial component focuses on how environmental harms disproportionately affect marginalized human communities and intersects with the oppression of non-human species.
* **Decolonization:**  This involves critically examining and dismantling the colonial legacies that have led to the exploitation of both humans and nature.

> **EXPERT TIP:**  Understanding relationality is key to grasping multispecies justice. It's about recognizing that our actions have consequences far beyond ourselves.
