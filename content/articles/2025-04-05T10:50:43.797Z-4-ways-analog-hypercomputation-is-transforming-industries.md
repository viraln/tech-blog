## What is Analog Hypercomputation? Stepping Beyond Turing's Limits

At its core, hypercomputation refers to computational models that can perform tasks beyond the capabilities of a Turing machine. The Turing machine, a theoretical model of computation, forms the basis of modern digital computers. However, it has inherent limitations. Certain mathematical problems, known as undecidable problems, are fundamentally unsolvable by any Turing machine. Analog hypercomputation offers a potential path to overcome these limitations.

Analog computation utilizes continuous physical quantities, such as voltage, current, or mechanical displacement, to represent and process information. Unlike digital computers that operate on discrete bits (0s and 1s), analog systems leverage the inherent properties of physical systems to perform computations. This allows for the potential to represent and manipulate infinite precision numbers, a key ingredient in exceeding Turing machine limits.
