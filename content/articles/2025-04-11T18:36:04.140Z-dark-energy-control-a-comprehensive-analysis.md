## Understanding Dark Energy: The Theory Explained

Dark energy, first observed in the late 1990s, is a hypothetical form of energy that permeates all of space and tends to accelerate the expansion of the universe.  Unlike ordinary matter and dark matter, which exert gravitational attraction, dark energy exhibits a repulsive gravitational effect.  Its nature remains largely unknown; however, the leading theory suggests it's a property of space itself, a "cosmological constant" as Einstein originally proposed, or perhaps a dynamic field with evolving properties. ![Illustration of the accelerating expansion of the universe due to dark energy](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1451847251646-8a6c0dd1510c?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

> **Did you know?**  Dark energy constitutes approximately 68% of the total energy density of the universe, dwarfing both ordinary matter and dark matter combined!

The current understanding is based primarily on observations of distant supernovae, which appear dimmer than expected, indicating an accelerated expansion. This acceleration implies a repulsive force counteracting gravity, a force we call dark energy.  Further evidence comes from the cosmic microwave background radiation and large-scale structure surveys.
