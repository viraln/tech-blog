## What is a Room-Temperature Superconductor?

A superconductor is a material that exhibits zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature.  This means electricity can flow through it indefinitely without losing any energy.  Traditionally, these critical temperatures have been extremely low, requiring expensive and complex cooling systems using liquid nitrogen or helium.  A room-temperature superconductor, as the name suggests, achieves superconductivity at or near room temperature, eliminating the need for these cumbersome and energy-intensive cooling methods.  This seemingly small change unlocks a world of previously unimaginable possibilities. ![Microscopic image of a superconductor material](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1467043153537-a4fba2cd39ef?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
