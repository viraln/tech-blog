## Experiments Proving Non-Locality: From Theory to Reality

While initially a theoretical concept, numerous experiments have provided increasingly robust evidence for non-locality. These experiments typically involve creating pairs of entangled photons and then measuring their properties, such as polarization, at distant locations.

Notable experiments include:

*   **The Aspect Experiment (1982):** Alain Aspect's groundbreaking experiment provided strong evidence against local realism, a philosophical viewpoint that assumes that objects have definite properties independent of observation and that influences cannot travel faster than light.
*   **Quantum Delayed-Choice Experiments:** These experiments demonstrate that the act of measurement itself can retroactively influence the past state of a particle, further blurring the lines between cause and effect.
*   **Satellite-Based Entanglement Distribution:** Recent experiments have successfully demonstrated quantum entanglement over vast distances using satellites, paving the way for secure global quantum communication networks.
