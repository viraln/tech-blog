## Atomic Design vs. Modular Design: A Clear Comparison

While both Atomic and Modular Design emphasize reusability, there are key differences.  Atomic Design focuses on a granular, hierarchical breakdown of components, while Modular Design emphasizes the creation of self-contained, reusable modules that might encompass several levels of the Atomic hierarchy.

| Feature          | Atomic Design                               | Modular Design                                    |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Granularity      | Highly granular (atoms, molecules, etc.)    | Less granular, larger, self-contained modules    |
| Hierarchy        | Strict, hierarchical structure              | Less strict hierarchy, more flexible             |
| Reusability      | Extremely high, at all levels               | High, but often at a higher level of abstraction |
| Complexity       | Can be more complex to implement initially   | Simpler to implement initially, potentially less scalable |
| Maintainability | Excellent                                    | Good, but can become challenging with growth     |
