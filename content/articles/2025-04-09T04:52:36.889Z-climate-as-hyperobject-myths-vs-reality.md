## What is a Climate Hyperobject?

The term "hyperobject," coined by philosopher Timothy Morton, describes entities so massively distributed in time and space that they are difficult, if not impossible, for humans to grasp fully. ## Climate Hyperobject Theory: Key Concepts

Morton's work emphasizes several key characteristics of hyperobjects that are particularly relevant to climate change:

* **Viscosity:**  The feeling of being stuck, entangled, and overwhelmed by the scale of the problem.
* **Nonlocality:**  The hyperobject's existence transcends specific locations; it's everywhere and nowhere at once.
* **Temporal Dispersion:**  Its effects unfold across vast timescales, impacting future generations.
* **Phasedness:**  We experience the hyperobject through its various manifestations (rising sea levels, extreme weather events), but never the whole thing at once.
