## Understanding Neural Feedback: More Than Just Brainwaves

Neural feedback, also known as neurofeedback or EEG biofeedback, is a type of biofeedback that uses real-time displays of brainwave activity to train individuals to self-regulate their brain function.  ![A diagram showing brainwave activity being monitored and displayed on a screen](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1624640477805-064c4e3b2c93?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

It works by providing the brain with information about its own activity, allowing individuals to learn to modify patterns associated with undesirable states like anxiety or ADHD.  This process harnesses the brain's inherent plasticity, its ability to reorganize and adapt throughout life.

> **DID YOU KNOW?**  The first documented use of EEG biofeedback dates back to the 1960s, but its applications and sophistication have dramatically increased in recent years.

## The Science Behind Neurofeedback: How Does it Work?

Neurofeedback employs electroencephalography (EEG) to measure brainwave activity.  Sensors placed on the scalp detect electrical signals, which are then processed and displayed in real-time.  A trained therapist guides the individual to modify their brainwave patterns by providing feedback through visual or auditory cues. For example, a patient might see a video game character move only when their brainwaves are within a desired range.  This process strengthens the desired brainwave patterns, improving self-regulation and potentially alleviating symptoms of various conditions.  ![A person undergoing a neurofeedback session with sensors attached to their head](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1550607326-2df38662b7dc?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
