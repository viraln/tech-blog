## 1.  Modular Design: Building with Lego Bricks, Not Monoliths

Traditional engineering often favors monolithic systems – large, complex, and tightly integrated structures.  Changes require extensive rework, leading to delays and increased costs.  Flexibility engineering flips this script by embracing *modular design*.  Imagine building with Lego bricks: each module is self-contained, independent, and easily interchangeable.  ![Illustration of a complex system built from modular components](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1451187580459-43490279c0fa?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
*Illustration of a complex system built from modular components*
*Photo by [Christopher Burns](https://unsplash.com/@christopher__burns) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/@christopher__burns)*

This approach offers several key advantages:

* 🔑 **Easier Upgrades and Maintenance:**  Replacing or upgrading a single module doesn't require a system-wide overhaul.
* ⚡ **Faster Time-to-Market:**  New features and functionalities can be added quickly by integrating new modules.
* ✅ **Reduced Risk:**  Failures are contained within individual modules, preventing cascading system failures.

> **EXPERT TIP:** When designing modular systems, prioritize well-defined interfaces between modules to ensure seamless integration and prevent unforeseen compatibility issues.
