## The Origin of the Oceanic Feeling

The term "oceanic feeling" was first coined by French Nobel laureate Romain Rolland in a letter to Sigmund Freud. Rolland described it as a sensation of "eternity, a feeling as of something limitless, unbounded—as it were, 'oceanic.'" Freud, while acknowledging the power of this feeling, attributed it to a regression to an infantile state of ego development. He saw it as a yearning for the undifferentiated unity experienced in early infancy.

However, the concept has evolved far beyond Freud's initial interpretation. ## Carl Jung and the Collective Unconscious

Carl Jung, a contemporary of Freud, offered a different perspective on the oceanic feeling. While he acknowledged the infantile roots, Jung also connected it to the *collective unconscious* – a universal reservoir of archetypes and shared human experiences. Jung believed that the oceanic feeling could be a manifestation of tapping into this collective unconscious, allowing individuals to access a deeper understanding of themselves and the world around them.

For Jung, the ocean itself served as a powerful symbol of the unconscious. Its vastness, depth, and mysterious currents mirrored the uncharted territories of the human psyche. The oceanic experience, therefore, could be seen as a journey into the depths of one's own being, leading to greater self-awareness and individuation.
