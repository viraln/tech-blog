## 1. The Core Principle: Moving Beyond Either/Or to Both/And

Complementarity thinking, rooted in complementarity theory, challenges the conventional wisdom that only one perspective can be true. Instead, it posits that seemingly contradictory viewpoints can coexist and offer unique insights when considered together. It's about embracing paradox, not resolving it through choosing one side over the other. Think of it like wave-particle duality in physics – light can behave as both a wave and a particle, depending on how it's observed. Similarly, many situations in life and business require us to hold seemingly opposing ideas in our minds simultaneously to gain a more complete understanding.

This approach stands in stark contrast to traditional linear thinking, which often forces us to choose between two options. Consider a business deciding between focusing on short-term profits versus long-term sustainability. Linear thinking might frame this as an "either/or" decision. Complementarity thinking, however, encourages exploring how both can be achieved simultaneously – perhaps through sustainable practices that attract environmentally conscious consumers and ultimately boost long-term profitability. This is a core tenet of the systems thinking approach.
