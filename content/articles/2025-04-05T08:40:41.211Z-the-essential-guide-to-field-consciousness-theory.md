## The Roots of Field Consciousness: From Jung to Sheldrake

The idea of a collective or universal consciousness has been around for centuries. Carl Jung's concept of the "collective unconscious" is a key precursor. Jung believed that all humans share a reservoir of universal archetypes and symbols accessible through dreams and intuition.

However, the modern formulation of field consciousness owes much to the work of Rupert Sheldrake. Sheldrake's theory of "morphic resonance" proposes that habits and patterns of behavior are transmitted across time and space through morphic fields. This means that when something is learned by one individual or group, it becomes easier for others to learn it, even if they are not directly connected. This explains, for example, how scientific discoveries often seem to occur simultaneously in different parts of the world.

Morphic resonance provides a mechanism for how the consciousness field might operate, suggesting that repeated thoughts, emotions, and behaviors create pathways within the field, making them more accessible to others. This is a core component of understanding how the consciousness field works.
