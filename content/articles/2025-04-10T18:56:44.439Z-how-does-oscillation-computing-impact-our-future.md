## What is Oscillation Computing?

Oscillation computing utilizes the frequency and phase relationships of oscillating systems, such as coupled oscillators, to perform computations. Unlike digital computing, which relies on binary (0 and 1) representations, oscillation computing leverages the continuous nature of oscillations.  Information is encoded in the frequency, amplitude, and phase of these oscillations, allowing for parallel processing and potentially circumventing the Von Neumann bottleneck. ## Oscillation Computing vs Digital Computing: A Detailed Comparison

The core difference lies in how information is represented and processed. Digital computing uses discrete binary values (0 and 1), while oscillation computing employs continuous variables like frequency and phase.  This fundamental difference leads to distinct advantages and disadvantages. Digital computing excels in precise, sequential operations, while oscillation computing shines in parallel processing of analog signals.
