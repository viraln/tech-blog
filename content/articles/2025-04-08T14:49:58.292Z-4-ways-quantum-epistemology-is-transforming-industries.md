## 3. Re-evaluating Data Interpretation through the Quantum Measurement Problem

The quantum measurement problem is a fundamental question in quantum mechanics: How does a quantum system, existing in a superposition of states, "collapse" into a definite state when measured? This seemingly abstract problem has profound implications for how we interpret data and construct knowledge.

*   **Observer Effect:** The act of observing a quantum system inevitably affects its state. Similarly, in data analysis, the way we collect and analyze data can influence the results.

*   **Subjective Reality:** Quantum mechanics suggests that reality is not entirely objective but is influenced by the observer. This challenges the traditional notion of objective truth in data interpretation.

*   **Quantum Bayesianism (QBism):** This interpretation of quantum mechanics views quantum states as subjective degrees of belief rather than objective properties of the system.
