## Understanding the Fundamentals of Flocking Algorithms

The cornerstone of any flocking system is the flocking algorithm.  While many exist, the most widely known and implemented is Craig Reynolds' Boids algorithm. This algorithm relies on three simple rules, elegantly mimicking complex emergent behavior:

* **Separation:** Avoid collisions with nearby flockmates.
* **Alignment:** Steer towards the average heading of nearby flockmates.
* **Cohesion:** Steer towards the average position of nearby flockmates.

These rules, when applied iteratively to each individual "boid" (a virtual bird), generate the characteristic flocking patterns.  ![Illustration of the three Boids rules](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476357471311-43c0db9fb2b4?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

##  Boids Algorithm Implementation: A Practical Guide

Implementing a basic Boids algorithm is surprisingly straightforward, even for beginners.  Here's a step-by-step guide using pseudocode:

**Step 1:** Define the Boid class.  This includes position, velocity, and acceleration vectors.

**Step 2:** Implement the three core rules (Separation, Alignment, Cohesion) as separate functions, each calculating a steering vector based on nearby boids.

**Step 3:** In the main update loop:
    1.  Calculate the steering vector for each boid by summing the vectors from the three rules.
    2.  Update the boid's velocity and position based on the calculated steering vector.
    3.  Wrap the boid's position around the screen edges (for continuous movement).
