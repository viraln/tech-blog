## Facial Attractiveness Perception: The Role of Symmetry and Averageness

Research consistently points to two key features influencing facial attractiveness: symmetry and averageness.  Symmetrical faces are generally perceived as more attractive because they signal good health and genetic fitness.  A perfectly symmetrical face is rare; slight asymmetries are typically present.  Averageness, on the other hand, refers to the proximity of a face to the average facial features within a population.  Faces closer to this average tend to be judged as more attractive. ![Examples of symmetrical and asymmetrical faces](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1498843053639-170ff2122f35?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

This phenomenon is explained by the "prototype theory" of attractiveness, which suggests that our brains have an internal prototype of an "average" face.  The closer a face is to this prototype, the more easily and efficiently our brains process it, leading to a positive emotional response.
