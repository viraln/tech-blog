## 1. The Subjective Nature of Quantum States: Reality as Personal Experience

The cornerstone of QBism lies in its assertion that quantum states are not objective properties of the physical world. Instead, they represent *personal beliefs* or probabilities held by an individual agent. This is a dramatic departure from interpretations like the Copenhagen interpretation, which, while acknowledging the role of measurement, still implies an underlying objective reality that is being disturbed by the act of observation.

In QBism, a quantum state, mathematically represented as a density operator, encodes an agent's subjective expectations about the outcomes of future measurements. When an agent performs a measurement, they update their beliefs based on the observed outcome, using the familiar Bayesian rule. This update is not a passive reflection of an external reality, but an *active construction* of their personal experience.

## 2. Born's Rule as a Normative Statement: A Guide to Consistent Decision-Making

Born's rule, a central tenet of quantum mechanics, connects quantum states to probabilities of measurement outcomes. In standard interpretations, it's typically seen as a descriptive law of nature, telling us how often we can expect to see certain outcomes in repeated experiments. QBism takes a different approach.

QBism views Born's rule as a *normative* statement, a guide for rational decision-making. It doesn't tell us what *will* happen, but rather what *should* happen if we want to maintain consistency between our beliefs and our actions.

To understand this, consider a simple analogy. Suppose you're offered a bet on the outcome of a quantum measurement. If you assign a particular quantum state to the system, Born's rule tells you how to price that bet rationally. Deviating from Born's rule would expose you to potential inconsistencies and make you vulnerable to exploitation.

## 3. The Quantum World as a World of Experience: Going Beyond Physical Primitives

Perhaps the most radical aspect of QBism is its assertion that quantum mechanics is not fundamentally about the behavior of physical objects. Instead, it's about the structure of *experience* itself.

In this view, the quantum world is not a collection of particles and fields evolving in a deterministic manner. It's a realm of possibilities and potentialities, where agents interact with the world through their actions and observations, constantly updating their beliefs and shaping their own personal narratives.

This doesn't mean that there's no objective reality. QBism acknowledges that there's something "out there" that causes our experiences. However, it argues that quantum mechanics doesn't provide a direct description of this objective reality. Instead, it provides a framework for understanding how our experiences are structured and how we can navigate the world in a consistent and rational way.
