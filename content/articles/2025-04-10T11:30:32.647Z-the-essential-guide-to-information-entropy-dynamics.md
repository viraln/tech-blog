## What is Information Entropy?  A Simple Explanation

Information entropy, a cornerstone of information theory, measures the uncertainty or randomness within a system.  Unlike thermodynamic entropy (which measures disorder at a physical level), information entropy quantifies the unpredictability of information.  The higher the entropy, the greater the uncertainty.  Imagine flipping a coin: a fair coin (50/50 chance of heads or tails) has high information entropy. A rigged coin, always landing on heads, has low information entropy because the outcome is predictable. ![A graphic illustrating the concept of high vs. low information entropy using coin flips](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1704022472896-c7f2423b3ce7?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

## The Mathematical Foundation of Information Entropy

The fundamental equation for calculating information entropy, developed by Claude Shannon, is:

H(X) = - Σ P(xi) log₂ P(xi)

Where:

* H(X) represents the entropy of a random variable X.
* P(xi) is the probability of the i-th outcome.
* log₂ is the logarithm base 2.

This equation quantifies the average amount of information needed to describe the outcome of a random variable.  ![The Shannon entropy equation displayed prominently](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1564760290292-23341e4df6ec?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
