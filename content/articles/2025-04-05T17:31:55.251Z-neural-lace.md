Neural lace is a mesh-like structure designed to be implanted into the brain, creating a direct interface between neural tissue and external computing devices. Unlike traditional BCIs that often involve bulky electrodes and invasive surgery, neural lace aims for a minimally invasive approach. The "lace" is typically composed of biocompatible materials, such as polymers or nanowires, that can conform to the brain's intricate folds.

The key advantage of neural lace lies in its ability to establish a high-bandwidth, bidirectional communication channel with neurons. This allows for:

*   **Neural Recording:** Monitoring brain activity with unprecedented precision.
*   **Neural Stimulation:** Activating specific neurons to influence behavior, mood, or cognitive function.
*   **Data Transfer:** Uploading information to the brain or downloading thoughts and experiences to external devices.

Current neural lace designs often involve injecting the mesh into the brain using a minimally invasive procedure. Once implanted, the lace unfolds and integrates with the surrounding neural tissue. The external interface is then connected wirelessly or through a small port in the skull.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Biocompatibility is paramount. The materials used in neural lace must be non-toxic and minimize the risk of inflammation or rejection by the body's immune system.
