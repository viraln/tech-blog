##  The Fundamentals of Wavefront Reconstruction: A Quick Refresher

Before we delve into the future, let's briefly revisit the basics. Wavefront reconstruction is the process of determining the shape of a wavefront—the surface of constant phase of a wave—after it has passed through a distorting medium. This distortion can be caused by atmospheric turbulence (in astronomy), optical imperfections in lenses, or biological tissues (in ophthalmology).  ![Diagram showing a distorted wavefront before and after reconstruction](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1613477757024-fb6d3fd3c0a9?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
*Diagram showing a distorted wavefront before and after reconstruction*
*Photo by [Олег Мороз](https://unsplash.com/@tengyart) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/@tengyart)*.

The most common method employs a wavefront sensor, such as the ubiquitous Shack-Hartmann sensor, to measure the local slopes of the wavefront.  These measurements are then used by wavefront reconstruction algorithms to estimate the overall wavefront shape.  The reconstructed wavefront allows for the correction of distortions, leading to sharper images, improved laser beam quality, and more accurate measurements.  Understanding this process is crucial to appreciating the advancements to come.

> **Did you know?**  The principles of wavefront reconstruction were initially developed in the field of astronomy to compensate for atmospheric blurring, significantly enhancing the resolution of ground-based telescopes.
