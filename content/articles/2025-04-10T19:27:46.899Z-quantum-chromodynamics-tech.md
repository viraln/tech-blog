## What is Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)?

Quantum Chromodynamics is the theoretical framework describing the strong interaction, one of the four fundamental forces in nature.  It explains how quarks, the fundamental constituents of protons and neutrons, are bound together by gluons, the force-carrying particles.  Unlike electromagnetism, which involves only one type of charge, QCD involves three "color charges" – red, green, and blue – and their anti-colors. This intricate interplay of colors and anti-colors dictates the behavior of hadrons, particles made of quarks (like protons and neutrons).  ![Illustration of quarks and gluons interacting](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531297484001-80022131f5a1?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

> **Did you know?** The name "chromodynamics" comes from the Greek word "chroma," meaning color, reflecting the crucial role of color charges in QCD.

Understanding QCD is challenging because the strong force becomes incredibly strong at low energies, making analytical solutions difficult.  This is why computational methods, particularly lattice QCD, play a vital role in extracting predictions from the theory.
