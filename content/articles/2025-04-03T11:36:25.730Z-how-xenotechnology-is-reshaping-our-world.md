## What is Xenotechnology? Unveiling the Power of Programmable Organisms

Xenotechnology, at its core, is the design and construction of biological machines from living cells. It goes beyond traditional genetic engineering by utilizing cells from organisms not typically used in bioengineering, often sourced from unusual or evolutionarily distant species. The most prominent example is the *Xenopus laevis* frog, whose embryonic cells form the basis of the groundbreaking "xenobots." These aren't robots in the conventional sense, but rather programmable organisms with the potential to perform complex tasks.

> **EXPERT TIP:** The "xeno" prefix signifies "foreign" or "strange," highlighting the use of non-traditional biological building blocks in this technology.

The key distinction between xenobots and traditional robots lies in their composition and capabilities. Robots are typically made of metal, plastic, and electronic components, while xenobots are entirely biological. This gives them unique advantages: they are biodegradable, biocompatible, and can self-heal. Furthermore, their behavior can be programmed through careful design and manipulation of their cellular structure.
