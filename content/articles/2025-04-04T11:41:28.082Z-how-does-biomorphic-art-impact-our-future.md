## What Exactly *Is* Biomorphic Art?

Let's start with the biomorphic art definition. At its core, biomorphic art, also known as Organic art, is characterized by the use of forms and imagery derived from nature. Think flowing lines reminiscent of plant growth, shapes that echo cellular structures, and compositions that evoke the dynamism of natural processes. It's not about literal representation; rather, it's about capturing the *essence* of organic forms, the feeling of life and growth. Artists who embrace this style often seek to emulate the inherent beauty and efficiency found in the natural world.

The term "biomorphic" comes from the Greek words "bios" (life) and "morphe" (form). It was first coined in the 1930s to describe the abstract art of artists like Jean Arp, Joan Miró, and Henry Moore, whose works featured shapes that resembled living organisms. This style is distinctly different from geometric abstraction, which relies on rigid shapes and precise lines. Biomorphic art embraces fluidity, asymmetry, and a sense of continuous movement.
