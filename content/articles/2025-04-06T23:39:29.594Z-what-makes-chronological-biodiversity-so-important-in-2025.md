## Tracking Biodiversity Through Time: The Methodologies

Tracking biodiversity through time requires a combination of different methodologies, including:

*   **Paleontology:** Analyzing fossil records to reconstruct past ecosystems and track species evolution and extinction.
*   **Historical Ecology:** Examining historical documents, such as diaries, maps, and photographs, to reconstruct past landscapes and track changes in species distributions and abundance.
*   **Dendrochronology:** Using tree rings to reconstruct past climate conditions and track changes in forest composition and growth rates.
*   **Molecular Phylogenetics:** Using DNA sequences to reconstruct evolutionary relationships among species and estimate the timing of speciation events.
*   **Environmental DNA (eDNA) analysis:** Analyzing DNA extracted from environmental samples (e.g., soil, water) to identify the species present in a given area at a given time. This method is becoming increasingly powerful for reconstructing past biodiversity, especially in aquatic environments.

Combining these different methodologies allows us to create a more comprehensive and accurate picture of biodiversity change over time.
