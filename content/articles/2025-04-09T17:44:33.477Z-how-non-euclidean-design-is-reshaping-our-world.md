## Understanding Non-Euclidean Geometry: Beyond the Flat Plane

Before diving into design applications, let's briefly explore the mathematical foundation.  Euclidean geometry, the geometry we learn in school, operates on a flat, two-dimensional plane and assumes parallel lines never meet.  Non-Euclidean geometry, however, breaks these rules, exploring spaces with different curvature.  This leads to two primary types:

* **Hyperbolic Geometry:** Imagine a saddle; this geometry describes surfaces with negative curvature, where parallel lines diverge.  This creates a sense of infinite expansion and often results in designs with intricate, fractal-like patterns. ![Example of a hyperbolic surface](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476357471311-43c0db9fb2b4?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

* **Spherical Geometry:**  Think of a sphere; this geometry describes surfaces with positive curvature, where all lines eventually intersect. This leads to designs that feel enclosed, rounded, and often symmetrical. ![Example of a spherical surface](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1534670007418-fbb7f6cf32c3?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
