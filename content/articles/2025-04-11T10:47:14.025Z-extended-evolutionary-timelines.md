## Punctuated Equilibrium vs. Gradualism: Two Perspectives on Evolutionary Change

Two dominant theories attempt to explain the pace of evolutionary change: punctuated equilibrium and gradualism.  Gradualism posits that evolution occurs steadily and gradually over long periods.  Punctuated equilibrium, on the other hand, proposes that evolution proceeds in fits and starts, with periods of rapid change interspersed with long periods of relative stability. ![A side-by-side comparison of the two models, illustrating their differences.](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531178625044-cc2a0fb353a9?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

The debate between these two models is far from settled.  Many evolutionary changes appear gradual when examined over vast timescales, but closer inspection often reveals periods of rapid diversification or extinction.  The "extended evolutionary synthesis" aims to integrate aspects of both models, recognizing the influence of factors like developmental bias and epigenetic inheritance on evolutionary trajectories.
