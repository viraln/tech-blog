## Understanding the Wave Function Collapse Algorithm

At its core, the Wave Function Collapse (WFC) algorithm is a procedural generation technique. Originally developed for creating tile-based textures, it works by defining a set of "tiles" and the rules that govern how they can connect to each other. Think of it like a sophisticated jigsaw puzzle where the pieces can have multiple possible connections, but only certain combinations are valid.

The algorithm starts with a grid of "uncollapsed" cells, each representing a superposition of all possible tiles. It then iteratively collapses these cells, one by one, based on the constraints imposed by the neighboring cells. As one cell collapses to a specific tile, it influences the possible states of its neighbors, triggering a cascade of collapses until the entire grid is resolved.

This process, while seemingly simple, can generate incredibly complex and varied patterns. The key to its power lies in the carefully designed constraints, which dictate the emergent behavior of the system. This is the foundation upon which the "Wave Function Collapse Mind" is built.
