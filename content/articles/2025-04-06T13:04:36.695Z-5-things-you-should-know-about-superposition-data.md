## 1. Understanding the Core Principle: What is Superposition?

At its essence, superposition refers to the ability of a quantum system, like a qubit (quantum bit), to exist in multiple states *simultaneously*. This is a stark contrast to classical bits in traditional computing, which can only be either 0 or 1. Imagine a coin spinning in the air: it's neither heads nor tails until it lands. That's a simple, albeit imperfect, analogy for superposition.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Don't think of superposition as simply being "both 0 and 1 at the same time." It's more accurate to say the qubit exists in a *probabilistic combination* of both states.

This probabilistic nature is key. A qubit in superposition is described by a wave function that specifies the probability amplitude of being in the 0 state and the probability amplitude of being in the 1 state. When we measure the qubit, the superposition collapses, and we observe either 0 or 1, with the probability determined by the wave function.
