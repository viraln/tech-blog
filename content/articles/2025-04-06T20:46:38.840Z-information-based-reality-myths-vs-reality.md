## The Evidence: Quantum Physics and the Informational Universe

The evidence for IBR isn't conclusive, but several key findings in quantum physics lend credence to the idea:

*   **Quantum Superposition:** Particles can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. This suggests that reality is only defined when information is accessed or observed.
*   **Quantum Entanglement:** Two particles can be linked in such a way that they share the same fate, no matter how far apart they are. This suggests a non-local connection that transcends traditional notions of space and time, potentially mediated by information.
*   **The Observer Effect:** The act of observing a quantum system changes its behavior. This highlights the role of information and measurement in shaping reality.

These quantum phenomena suggest that information plays a more fundamental role than previously thought. Physicists like John Archibald Wheeler, with his concept of "it from bit," have long argued that information is the foundation of reality.

⚡**Key Takeaway:** Quantum physics provides intriguing hints that information is not just a description of reality, but a crucial ingredient in its creation.

## Simulated Reality: A Branch of IBR or a Separate Idea?

The Simulated Reality hypothesis, popularized by philosophers like Nick Bostrom, proposes that it's statistically likely that we are living in a computer simulation created by a more advanced civilization. While often associated with IBR, it's important to distinguish between the two.

Simulated Reality *assumes* a pre-existing reality that is being simulated. IBR, on the other hand, posits that information *is* the fundamental reality, regardless of whether it's being simulated or not. A simulated reality could be built upon an information-based reality, but the core concept of IBR stands independently.
