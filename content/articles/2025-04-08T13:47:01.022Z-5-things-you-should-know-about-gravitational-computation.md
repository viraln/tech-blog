## 2. Gravitational Search Algorithm (GSA): A Popular Implementation

The Gravitational Search Algorithm (GSA) is one of the most well-known and widely used implementations of gravitational computation. Developed in 2009, GSA leverages the laws of gravity and motion to iteratively improve a population of candidate solutions.

Here’s a simplified breakdown of how GSA works:

**Step 1:** Initialize a population of agents (solutions) randomly within the search space. Assign each agent a mass based on its fitness (cost function value).

**Step 2:** Calculate the gravitational force exerted on each agent by all other agents. The force is proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

**Step 3:** Calculate the acceleration of each agent based on the net force acting on it.

**Step 4:** Update the velocity and position of each agent based on its acceleration.

**Step 5:** Evaluate the fitness of the new positions of the agents.

**Step 6:** Repeat steps 2-5 until a termination criterion is met (e.g., maximum number of iterations, desired accuracy achieved).

✅ **Key Benefits of GSA:**

*   **Simplicity:** The algorithm is relatively easy to understand and implement.
*   **Few parameters:** GSA has fewer parameters to tune compared to some other optimization algorithms.
*   **Global search capability:** Its inherent exploration mechanism helps it escape local optima.

However, GSA also has its limitations. Early convergence, sensitivity to parameter settings, and computational cost for high-dimensional problems are common challenges.

## Common Challenges and Solutions

*   **Parameter Sensitivity:** The performance of GSA can be sensitive to the choice of parameters, such as the gravitational constant and the population size.

*   **Computational Cost:** GSA can be computationally expensive for large-scale problems, especially when evaluating the fitness function is time-consuming.
