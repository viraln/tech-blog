Quantum Decision Making (QDM) is a cognitive science framework that applies the mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics to model human decision-making. It's not about using quantum computers (though that's a related field) but rather about using quantum probability and other quantum concepts to better understand and predict how people make choices. Classical decision theory assumes that people have well-defined preferences and make rational choices based on maximizing expected utility. QDM addresses these limitations by incorporating concepts like superposition, entanglement, and interference, which more accurately reflect the complexities of human cognition.

## Core Principles of Quantum Decision Making

Understanding the core principles of QDM is crucial for grasping its power and potential. Here are some key concepts:

*   **Superposition:** In quantum mechanics, a particle can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. Similarly, in QDM, a decision-maker can hold multiple, conflicting beliefs or preferences simultaneously. This "superposition of states" reflects the uncertainty and ambiguity often present in real-world decisions.

*   **Interference:** Quantum interference occurs when different possibilities interact with each other, leading to constructive or destructive interference. In QDM, this means that considering one option can influence the evaluation of other options. This explains phenomena like the "attraction effect," where adding an inferior option can increase the attractiveness of a slightly better option.

*   **Entanglement:** Entanglement describes a situation where two or more particles are linked together in such a way that they share the same fate, no matter how far apart they are. In QDM, entanglement can model how different aspects of a decision (e.g., price and quality) are interconnected and influence each other.

*   **Quantum Probability:** Unlike classical probability, quantum probability doesn't assume that all possibilities are mutually exclusive. It allows for interference effects, leading to different probability distributions that can better explain observed choice patterns. Quantum probability in decision making often allows for more accurate predictions of human choices in ambiguous or uncertain situations.

*   **Contextuality:** QDM emphasizes that decisions are highly context-dependent. The same person might make different choices in different situations. This is captured by the mathematical framework of QDM, which incorporates contextual variables into the decision model.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Don't try to literally interpret quantum mechanics in the brain. QDM uses the *mathematics* of quantum mechanics as a tool to model cognitive processes, not as a claim about the physical implementation of those processes.
