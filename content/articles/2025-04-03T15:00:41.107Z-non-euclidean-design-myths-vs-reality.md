## What is Non-Euclidean Geometry, Really?

For over two millennia, Euclidean geometry, named after the ancient Greek mathematician Euclid, reigned supreme. It's the geometry we learn in school, based on axioms like parallel lines never meeting and the angles of a triangle always summing to 180 degrees. However, in the 19th century, mathematicians like Gauss, Bolyai, and Lobachevsky independently discovered that alternative geometric systems were possible – Non-Euclidean geometries.

These geometries challenge Euclid's parallel postulate. There are two primary types:

*   **Hyperbolic Geometry:** In hyperbolic space, parallel lines diverge, and the angles of a triangle sum to *less* than 180 degrees. Imagine a saddle shape – that's a rough approximation of hyperbolic space.

*   **Spherical Geometry:** In spherical space (also known as elliptic geometry), parallel lines *converge*, and the angles of a triangle sum to *more* than 180 degrees. Think of the surface of a sphere.
