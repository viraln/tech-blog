## The Foundations: Shannon's Information Theory and its Relevance

To truly understand Shannon Cosmology, we need to grasp the basics of Shannon's Information Theory. Claude Shannon, often called the "father of the Information Age," developed a mathematical theory to quantify information. His key concept is *entropy*, which measures the uncertainty or randomness of a system.

In information theory, entropy is not necessarily a bad thing. It represents the potential for new information to be created. A perfectly ordered system has zero entropy because there's nothing new to learn about it. A chaotic system has high entropy because there's a vast amount of potential information to be discovered.

Shannon's theory provides a way to quantify this information, allowing us to analyze and compare different systems. This is crucial for Shannon Cosmology, which seeks to apply these mathematical tools to the universe as a whole. The amount of information, I, needed to describe a physical system is related to the number of possible states, N, of the system through the equation: I = log2(N).
