## 1. The Computational Universe Hypothesis: Reality as Information Processing

At the heart of digital physics lies the Computational Universe Hypothesis. This hypothesis proposes that the universe is fundamentally a giant computer, and all physical processes are ultimately computations. This means that everything we perceive – matter, energy, space, and time – could be the result of underlying digital information being processed.

> **EXPERT TIP:** The strength of the Computational Universe Hypothesis lies in its ability to potentially reconcile quantum mechanics and general relativity, two pillars of modern physics that currently clash.

*   **Information as the Foundation:** Unlike traditional physics that prioritizes matter and energy, digital physics views information as the fundamental building block of reality.

## 3. The Simulation Hypothesis: Exploring the Implications of a Simulated Reality

The Simulation Hypothesis, popularized by philosopher Nick Bostrom, argues that at least one of the following propositions must be true:

1.  Humanity is very likely to go extinct before reaching a "posthuman" stage.
2.  Any posthuman civilization is extremely unlikely to run a significant number of simulations of their past.
3.  We are almost certainly living in a computer simulation.

If they do, and if they choose to run many such simulations, then the number of simulated realities would vastly outnumber the "real" reality. Therefore, statistically speaking, it's more likely that we are living in a simulation.
