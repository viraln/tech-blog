## Brain-Computer Interfaces: The Gateway to Telepathic Communication

The foundation of the telepathic internet lies in BCIs. These devices come in various forms, each with its own advantages and limitations:

*   **Invasive BCIs:** These require surgical implantation of electrodes directly into the brain. They offer the highest signal quality and precision but carry inherent risks associated with surgery.
*   **Non-invasive BCIs:** These use sensors placed on the scalp (like EEG headsets) to detect brain activity. They are safer and more accessible but offer lower signal resolution.
*   **Semi-invasive BCIs:** These involve minimally invasive techniques, such as inserting electrodes through blood vessels, offering a compromise between signal quality and risk.

Companies like Neuralink, Synchron, and OpenBCI are at the forefront of BCI development. Neuralink, for example, is working on implantable BCIs that can potentially treat neurological conditions and enhance human capabilities. Synchron has already achieved significant milestones with its Stentrode device, allowing paralyzed individuals to control digital devices with their thoughts. OpenBCI focuses on open-source BCI technology, making it more accessible to researchers and developers.
