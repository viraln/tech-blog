**2. DNA Computing:**  This approach utilizes DNA molecules as a computational substrate. **3. Optical Computing:**  Optical computers use photons instead of electrons for information processing. However, challenges remain in developing efficient optical logic gates and overcoming limitations in scalability.

**4. Biological Computing:** This emerging field leverages biological systems for computation. This includes using living cells, enzymes, or even entire organisms to perform computations.  It offers the potential for self-repairing, adaptable, and energy-efficient computers, particularly useful in bio-sensing and medical applications.  However, challenges include controlling the complexity of biological systems and ensuring reliable and reproducible results.

## Non-Von Neumann Architectures: Rethinking the Fundamentals

Traditional computers adhere to the Von Neumann architecture, characterized by a single processing unit accessing data from a separate memory location.  This architecture creates a bottleneck, limiting processing speed.  Non-Von Neumann architectures, such as neuromorphic computing and distributed computing, aim to overcome this limitation by distributing processing and memory across multiple units.  This parallel processing approach is particularly well-suited for handling massive datasets and complex computations.  ![A diagram comparing Von Neumann and Non-Von Neumann architectures.](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1540198163009-7afda7da2945?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
