## What is Synthetic DNA?  Understanding the Basics

Synthetic DNA, also known as artificial DNA, is DNA that is artificially synthesized in a laboratory rather than occurring naturally in an organism.  It's essentially a custom-designed sequence of nucleotides (adenine, guanine, cytosine, and thymine) assembled according to a specific plan. Unlike natural DNA extraction, which isolates existing DNA, synthetic DNA creation involves building it from the ground up. This allows scientists to create sequences that don't exist in nature, opening doors to unprecedented possibilities.  ![Microscopic image of synthetic DNA strands](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1681127910416-f18e60b0ba1a?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

> **Did you know?**  The first fully synthetic gene was created in 1970, paving the way for the sophisticated techniques we use today.

The process, often referred to as *de novo* DNA synthesis, involves various techniques, including the phosphoramidite method, which is the most widely used approach for creating short DNA sequences. For longer sequences, more advanced methods like Gibson assembly are employed. The cost of synthetic DNA has significantly decreased over the years, making it more accessible to researchers across various disciplines.
