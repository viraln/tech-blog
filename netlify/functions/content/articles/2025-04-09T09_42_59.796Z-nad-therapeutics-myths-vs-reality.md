Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is a coenzyme vital for cellular energy production and numerous metabolic processes.  As we age, our NAD+ levels naturally decline, potentially contributing to age-related decline in cellular function. ## The Science Behind NAD+ Therapeutics: How it Works

NAD+ therapies aim to increase NAD+ levels within the body.  This can be achieved through various methods, including:

* **NAD+ precursors:**  These are compounds that the body converts into NAD+, such as nicotinamide riboside (NR), nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), and nicotinic acid (NA). These are often taken as supplements.
* **Enzyme inhibitors:** These substances can indirectly increase NAD+ levels by inhibiting enzymes that break it down.
* **Direct NAD+ administration:**  This involves intravenous (IV) infusions of NAD+, bypassing the conversion process.  ![Illustration of different NAD+ delivery methods](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1655757929252-f09118cf6219?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
