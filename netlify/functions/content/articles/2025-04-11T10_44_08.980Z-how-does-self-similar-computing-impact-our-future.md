## The Mechanics of Self-Similar Algorithms:  Recursion and Iteration in Action

The core of self-similar computing lies in its algorithms. These aren't simple, linear instructions; instead, they employ recursion and iteration to achieve self-similarity.  Recursion involves a function calling itself, breaking down a problem into smaller, self-similar subproblems.  Iteration involves repeatedly applying a set of rules to refine a solution.  A simple example is the Mandelbrot set, a fractal generated by iterating a simple mathematical equation.
