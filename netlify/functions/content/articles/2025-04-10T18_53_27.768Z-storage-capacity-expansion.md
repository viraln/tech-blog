## Expanding Your Internal Storage:  Hard Drives & SSDs

For desktop and laptop users, expanding internal storage often involves upgrading your hard drive (HDD) or solid-state drive (SSD).  HDDs are generally more affordable per gigabyte, while SSDs offer significantly faster read/write speeds.  ![Comparison table of HDD vs. SSD features and costs](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1529333320936-e2193f4e3b32?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

**Step 1:** Identify your computer's compatibility. Check your motherboard's specifications to determine the type and size of drive it supports.
**Step 2:** Purchase a compatible drive.  Consider factors like capacity, speed (RPM for HDDs, interface speed for SSDs), and form factor (2.5" or 3.5").
**Step 3:**  Clone your existing drive (highly recommended to avoid data loss). Many utilities are available for this purpose.
**Step 4:** Install the new drive.  This usually involves opening your computer case and physically installing the drive, followed by configuring it in your operating system.

## Expanding Server Storage Capacity:  Enterprise-Level Solutions

For organizations with large-scale storage needs, expanding server storage capacity requires a more sophisticated approach.  This typically involves adding more storage arrays, upgrading existing storage controllers, or implementing advanced storage technologies like NVMe (Non-Volatile Memory Express) SSDs or Ceph distributed storage.  The cost of expanding server storage capacity can be substantial, but the benefits often outweigh the investment.
