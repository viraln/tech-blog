## The Oceanic Feeling: A Historical Perspective

The term "Oceanic Feeling" was first coined by French Nobel laureate Romain Rolland in a letter to Sigmund Freud. Rolland described it as a sensation of "eternity, a feeling as of something limitless, unbounded – as it were, 'oceanic.'" Freud, while acknowledging the feeling, attributed it to a regression to an infantile state, a dissolving of ego boundaries.

However, Carl Jung offered a different perspective, viewing the oceanic feeling as an experience of the collective unconscious, a shared reservoir of human experience and archetypes. Jung saw it not as a regression, but as a potential for growth and individuation, a reconnection with the deeper, more primal aspects of the self. The "Carl Jung oceanic feeling" is less about a simple feeling and more about a profound psychological experience.
