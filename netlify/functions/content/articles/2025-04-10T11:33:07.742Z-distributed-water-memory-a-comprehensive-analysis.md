## What is Distributed Water Memory?

Distributed water memory (DWM) proposes that water, even after being highly diluted, retains a subtle "memory" of the substances it previously contained. This memory isn't stored in the molecules of the original substance, which are long gone after extreme dilution, but rather in the structure of the water itself – its unique hydrogen-bonding network.  This network, influenced by the dissolved substance, is believed to persist and potentially exert biological effects.
