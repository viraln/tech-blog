## What is a Room-Temperature Superconductor?

Before diving into the revolutionary aspects, let's establish a foundational understanding.  A superconductor is a material that exhibits zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature.  This means electricity can flow through it indefinitely without losing energy – a stark contrast to traditional conductors like copper, which lose energy as heat.  The catch?  Most superconductors require extremely low temperatures (often near absolute zero), making them impractical for widespread use.  A room-temperature superconductor, however, operates at or near ambient temperatures, opening up a plethora of possibilities.
