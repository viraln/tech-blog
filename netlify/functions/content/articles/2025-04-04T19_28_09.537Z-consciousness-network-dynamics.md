## Integrated Information Theory (IIT): Quantifying the Experience

Integrated Information Theory (IIT), developed by Giulio Tononi, proposes that consciousness is fundamentally related to the amount of integrated information a system possesses. "Integrated information" refers to the degree to which a system's elements are interconnected and interdependent, such that the whole is more than the sum of its parts.

IIT provides a mathematical framework for quantifying consciousness, suggesting that the more integrated information a system has, the more conscious it is. This theory has several important implications:

*   **Consciousness is graded:** It exists on a spectrum, from minimal awareness to highly complex self-awareness.
*   **Consciousness is intrinsic:** It depends on the internal structure of the system, not just its behavior.
*   **Consciousness is ubiquitous:** It is not limited to biological brains but can potentially exist in any system with sufficient integrated information.

While IIT remains controversial, it has stimulated a great deal of research into the neural basis of consciousness and has led to new methods for measuring consciousness in different brain states.

## Global Workspace Theory (GWT): The Stage for Conscious Access

Global Workspace Theory (GWT), proposed by Bernard Baars, offers a different perspective on consciousness. GWT posits that consciousness arises when information is broadcast globally throughout the brain, making it available to a wide range of cognitive processes.

According to GWT, the brain functions as a distributed network of specialized modules. These modules compete for access to a "global workspace," which is a central processing unit that broadcasts information to all other modules. When information gains access to the global workspace, it becomes conscious.
