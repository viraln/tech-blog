Superposition, a fundamental principle of quantum mechanics, allows a quantum bit (qubit) to exist in multiple states (both 0 and 1) at the same time, unlike classical bits which are restricted to a single state.  Superposition data, therefore, refers to information encoded in these qubits, leveraging their inherent ability to represent a probabilistic combination of states. This isn't simply a matter of uncertainty; it's a fundamental characteristic that allows quantum computers to perform calculations in ways impossible for classical computers.  Think of it like a coin spinning in the air – before it lands, it's both heads and tails simultaneously.  Once it lands, the superposition collapses, and we get a definite outcome.
