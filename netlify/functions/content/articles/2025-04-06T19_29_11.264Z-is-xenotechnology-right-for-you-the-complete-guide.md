## What is Xenotechnology? Unveiling the Science of Living Robots

Xenotechnology is the design and engineering of biological systems, often starting from scratch, to perform specific tasks. At its heart lies the creation of **xenobots**, living robots built from biological cells, typically derived from frog embryos (specifically *Xenopus laevis*, hence the name). Unlike traditional robots made of metal and plastic, xenobots are entirely biological, biodegradable, and capable of self-healing. The field is rapidly evolving, pushing the boundaries of what's possible with biological matter.

![A microscopic image of a group of xenobots moving](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1451187580459-43490279c0fa?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

Xenobots are not just passive structures; they can be programmed to perform various functions, from delivering drugs within the body to collecting microplastics in the ocean. The 'programming' comes from their design, carefully crafted through algorithms that simulate different cell arrangements and predict their behavior. By selecting the right combination of cells and geometry, scientists can create xenobots with specific capabilities.

Did you know that some xenobots can even self-replicate under specific laboratory conditions? This breakthrough, achieved in 2021, sparked both excitement and debate about the potential and risks of this technology.
