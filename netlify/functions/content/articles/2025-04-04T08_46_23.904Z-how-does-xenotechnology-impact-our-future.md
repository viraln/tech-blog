## Understanding Xenotechnology: Building with Life

Xenotechnology, at its core, is the engineering of living organisms to perform specific tasks. Unlike traditional robotics, which relies on metal, plastic, and electronics, xenotechnology utilizes biological building blocks – specifically, living cells – to create functional machines. These machines, often referred to as "xenobots," are designed using sophisticated algorithms and then assembled from the cells of organisms like the African clawed frog ( *Xenopus laevis*, hence the name). This allows for the creation of biodegradable, self-healing, and adaptive robots that can operate in environments where traditional machines struggle.

Xenobots, on the other hand, can adapt to changing conditions and even self-repair. Their biological nature also makes them biocompatible, opening up possibilities for medical applications that are impossible with conventional robots.

## The Birth of Xenobots: A Programmable Living Machine

The journey to creating xenobots began with groundbreaking research at the University of Vermont and Tufts University. Scientists used an evolutionary algorithm to design thousands of different body shapes for the xenobots. These designs were then translated into instructions for assembling cells harvested from frog embryos. The remarkable aspect is that these cells, despite being removed from their natural context, spontaneously organized themselves into functional structures.

**Did You Know?** The original xenobots were designed to move autonomously, propelled by cilia – tiny hair-like structures – on their surface.
