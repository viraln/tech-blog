## Understanding the Basics: From Hypercubes to Architectural Models

Before delving into construction techniques, it's crucial to understand the fundamentals. A tesseract, also known as a hypercube, is the four-dimensional analogue of a cube. Just as a cube is constructed by folding a square into the third dimension, a tesseract can be imagined as folding a cube into the fourth dimension.

Building a physical tesseract model requires representing this 4D object in 3D space. This is typically done through:

*   **Wireframe Models:** These models use lines to represent the edges of the tesseract, providing a skeletal view of its structure.
*   **Net Diagrams:** Similar to unfolding a cube into a 2D net, a tesseract can be unfolded into a 3D net consisting of eight interconnected cubes.
*   **Projections:** Various projection techniques, such as Schlegel diagrams and perspective projections, can be used to create 2D or 3D representations of the tesseract.

These models, while not true tesseracts, allow us to explore the spatial relationships and geometric properties of their 4D counterpart. These are invaluable tools for architects and engineers exploring 4D building design.
