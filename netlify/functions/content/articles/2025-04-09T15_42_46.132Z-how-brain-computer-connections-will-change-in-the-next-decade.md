* 🔑 **Non-invasive BCIs:** These are widely accessible, utilizing EEG caps to detect brainwaves.  They're used in gaming, neurofeedback therapy, and assistive technologies.
* ⚡ **Invasive BCIs:**  These involve surgically implanting electrodes directly into the brain, offering higher resolution and more precise control.  They are primarily used in clinical settings for patients with severe paralysis or neurological disorders.
* ✅ **Partially Invasive BCIs:**  These represent a middle ground, with electrodes implanted within the skull but not directly into the brain tissue.  They offer a balance between invasiveness and signal quality.

##  How Does Brain-Computer Interface Work?

BCIs work by translating neural activity into commands that can be interpreted by external devices.  This involves several key steps:

1. **Signal Acquisition:**  Electrodes detect electrical signals produced by neurons.
2. **Signal Processing:**  Raw signals are filtered, amplified, and processed to remove noise and extract relevant information.
3. **Signal Decoding:**  Algorithms translate processed signals into meaningful commands.
4. **Command Execution:**  The decoded commands are used to control external devices, such as robotic limbs or computer cursors.

> **EXPERT TIP:**  The accuracy and speed of BCI systems depend heavily on the sophistication of signal processing and decoding algorithms.
