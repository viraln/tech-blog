## The Foundations: Understanding Non-Euclidean Geometry

For centuries, Euclidean geometry, based on the postulates of the ancient Greek mathematician Euclid, has been the cornerstone of our understanding of space. It governs our everyday experiences, from the straight lines of buildings to the angles of furniture. However, Non-Euclidean geometry, developed in the 19th century, breaks free from these constraints. It explores spaces where Euclid's parallel postulate – that parallel lines never meet – doesn't hold true.

There are two primary types of Non-Euclidean geometry:

*   **Hyperbolic Geometry:** Imagine a saddle-shaped surface. In hyperbolic geometry, parallel lines diverge, and the angles of a triangle add up to less than 180 degrees. This creates a sense of infinite space and distortion.

*   **Spherical Geometry:** Picture the surface of a sphere. In spherical geometry, parallel lines eventually intersect (think of lines of longitude converging at the poles), and the angles of a triangle add up to more than 180 degrees. This geometry represents a closed, finite space without boundaries.

Designing with curved space presents unique challenges and opportunities. Here are some practical considerations to keep in mind:

*   **Fabrication and Construction:** Building curved surfaces can be more complex and expensive than building traditional straight-line structures. Specialized fabrication techniques, such as CNC milling and 3D printing, may be required.
