## Decoding the Basics: How Does Molecular Data Storage Work?

Molecular digital storage, most commonly using DNA (DNA data storage), encodes digital information into the sequences of molecules. Think of it as rewriting the binary code of 0s and 1s into the four-letter alphabet of DNA: adenine (A), guanine (G), cytosine (C), and thymine (T).

The process generally involves these key steps:

**Step 1:** Converting digital data (0s and 1s) into a sequence of A, G, C, and T. Specific algorithms dictate how these conversions are made.

**Step 2:** Synthesizing strands of DNA with the encoded sequences. This is done using automated DNA synthesizers.

**Step 3:** Storing the synthesized DNA in a dehydrated form, allowing for long-term preservation.

**Step 4:** When the data needs to be retrieved, the DNA is sequenced using DNA sequencing technology.

**Step 5:** The DNA sequence is then decoded back into the original digital data.

This process, while complex, offers unparalleled density and longevity.
