Neural communication hinges on the neuron, the fundamental unit of the nervous system.  These specialized cells transmit information via electrochemical signals. ![Microscopic image of a neuron showing dendrites, axon, and synapses](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1556691421-cf15fe27a0b6?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

## Synaptic Transmission: The Chemical Crossroads of Neural Communication

The axon's journey doesn't end abruptly. Instead, it terminates at synapses – specialized junctions where the neuron communicates with another neuron, muscle cell, or gland.  At the synapse, the electrical signal is converted into a chemical signal. Neurotransmitters, chemical messengers, are released into the synaptic cleft – the tiny gap between neurons.  ![Illustration of a synapse showing neurotransmitter release and receptor binding](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1547190027-9156686aa2f0?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
*Illustration of a synapse showing neurotransmitter release and receptor binding*
*Photo by [Markus Spiske](https://unsplash.com/@markusspiske) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/@markusspiske)* These neurotransmitters bind to receptors on the receiving cell, triggering a new electrical signal in that cell. This intricate process of chemical-to-electrical conversion is called synaptic transmission.  The efficiency and speed of this process are critical for proper neural function.
