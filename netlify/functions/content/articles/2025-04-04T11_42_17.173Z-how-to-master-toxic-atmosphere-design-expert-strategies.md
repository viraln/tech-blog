## Common Hostile Design Features and Their Impact

Hostile design manifests in various forms, each with its own intended (and unintended) consequences. Understanding these features is essential for recognizing and critically evaluating their use.

*   **Anti-Homeless Spikes:** Perhaps the most controversial, these metal or concrete protrusions are designed to prevent people from sleeping in specific areas.
*   **Uncomfortable Benches:** Benches with armrests in the middle, sloping surfaces, or made of cold materials discourage prolonged sitting or sleeping.
*   **Water Sprinklers:** Timed sprinklers can deter people from lingering in parks or plazas during certain hours.
*   **Loud Noises/Sonic Deterrents:** High-frequency sounds, often inaudible to older adults, are used to discourage loitering by younger people.
*   **Limited Shelter:** Lack of awnings, covered seating, or public restrooms makes it difficult for people to seek refuge from the elements.
*   **Strategic Lighting:** Poor or excessively bright lighting can make areas feel unsafe or unwelcoming.

✅ **CHECKPOINT:** Consider the impact of these features on *all* members of the community, not just the target population.

## Urban Design for Control: Beyond the Obvious

Toxic atmosphere design isn't always about physical barriers. It can also involve subtle manipulations of space and perception.

*   **Surveillance:** The presence of CCTV cameras can deter crime and unwanted behavior, but also creates a sense of being watched.
*   **Wayfinding:** Confusing or poorly designed signage can discourage people from exploring certain areas.
*   **Gated Communities:** These create physical and social barriers, separating residents from the wider community.
*   **"Eyes on the Street":** Encouraging pedestrian traffic and creating vibrant streetscapes can deter crime by increasing natural surveillance. (This is a positive example of design influencing behavior).
