## What is Sensory Preference Mapping? Decoding Consumer Perception

Sensory preference mapping is a sophisticated sensory evaluation technique used to visually represent the relationship between the sensory attributes of products and consumer preferences. It goes beyond simple liking scores to uncover the specific sensory characteristics that drive consumer choice. By combining descriptive sensory analysis with consumer acceptance data, sensory preference mapping creates a "map" that highlights which sensory attributes are most liked by different consumer segments. This method falls under the umbrella of consumer sensory research and provides invaluable insights for product optimization, new product development, and targeted marketing strategies.

## Diving Deep: Sensory Evaluation Techniques Used in Sensory Preference Mapping

Sensory preference mapping relies on a combination of sensory evaluation techniques to gather comprehensive data. These techniques can be broadly categorized into descriptive analysis and consumer acceptance testing.

*   **Descriptive Analysis:** Trained sensory panelists evaluate products based on a standardized set of sensory attributes, such as aroma, taste, texture, and appearance. Common methods include Quantitative Descriptive Analysis (QDA) and Texture Profile Analysis (TPA). The goal is to create a detailed sensory profile of each product.

*   **Consumer Acceptance Testing:** Consumers evaluate products based on their overall liking or preference. Hedonic scales (e.g., 9-point hedonic scale) are commonly used to measure consumer acceptance. This data is then correlated with the descriptive analysis data to create the sensory preference map.

*   **Other Methods:** Projective mapping, also known as Napping®, involves asking consumers to position products on a physical space (e.g., a table) based on their perceived similarities and differences. This technique can provide valuable insights into consumer perception and can be used to complement traditional descriptive analysis and consumer acceptance testing.

## How to Conduct Sensory Preference Mapping: A Step-by-Step Guide

Conducting effective sensory preference mapping requires careful planning and execution. Here's a step-by-step guide to help you get started:

**Step 1:** Define Your Objectives. Clearly define the objectives of your sensory preference mapping study. What specific questions are you trying to answer? What product categories or attributes are you interested in exploring?

**Step 2:** Select Products and Sensory Attributes. Choose the products to be included in the study, including your own products, competitor products, and prototypes. Identify the key sensory attributes that are relevant to the product category and consumer perception.

**Step 3:** Recruit Participants. Recruit trained sensory panelists for descriptive analysis and consumers for acceptance testing. Ensure that participants are representative of your target audience.

**Step 4:** Conduct Descriptive Analysis. Train sensory panelists to evaluate products based on the selected sensory attributes. Collect data using standardized sensory evaluation techniques.

**Step 5:** Conduct Consumer Acceptance Testing. Collect data from consumers using hedonic scales or other preference measures.

**Step 6:** Analyze Data and Create Sensory Preference Map. Use statistical software to analyze the data and create a sensory preference map. Identify the sensory attributes that are most liked by different consumer segments.

**Step 7:** Interpret Results and Draw Conclusions. Interpret the sensory preference map and draw conclusions about consumer preferences and product positioning.

**Step 8:** Implement Findings. Use the insights from the sensory preference mapping study to inform product development, optimization, and marketing strategies.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Use appropriate statistical software for data analysis and visualization. Programs like XLSTAT, Senpaq, and R offer specialized tools for sensory preference mapping.
