## Understanding the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)

The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) is essentially the afterglow of the Big Bang. Discovered in 1965, it's a faint, uniform radiation permeating the entire universe. While incredibly faint, its presence suggests a vast reservoir of energy available for potential exploitation. Scientists estimate the CMB’s temperature to be approximately 2.7 Kelvin (-270.45°C or -454.81°F). While that's incredibly cold, the sheer volume of space it occupies makes the CMB a significant energy source in theory.
