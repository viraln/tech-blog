## What is Quantum Annealing Explained?

Quantum annealing is a type of quantum computation that leverages the principles of quantum mechanics to solve complex optimization problems. Unlike universal quantum computers aiming for general-purpose computation, quantum annealers are specialized devices designed to find the lowest energy state of a system – a process analogous to finding the optimal solution among many possibilities.  It achieves this by using quantum effects like superposition and tunneling to navigate a complex energy landscape far more efficiently than classical algorithms. ## Quantum Annealing Algorithm: A Deeper Dive

The core of quantum annealing lies in its algorithms.  Adiabatic Quantum Computation (AQC) is the most common approach.  It involves slowly changing the system's Hamiltonian (energy function) from an easily prepared initial state to the problem Hamiltonian, allowing the system to adiabatically follow the ground state.  This ensures the final state represents the optimal solution.  However, the "slow" change is a crucial parameter, and its optimization is a significant research area.
