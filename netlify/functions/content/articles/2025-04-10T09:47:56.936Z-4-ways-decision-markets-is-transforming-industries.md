## What Are Decision Markets and How Do They Work?

Decision markets, also sometimes referred to as prediction markets (though there are key differences we'll explore later), are structured systems where participants buy and sell shares representing the likelihood of specific future events.  The price of these shares fluctuates based on supply and demand, reflecting the collective assessment of the market participants.  Unlike traditional prediction markets primarily focused on speculative trading, decision markets are designed to generate actionable intelligence for organizations.  They tap into the diverse knowledge and perspectives within a company or across a wider network to improve decision-making across numerous areas. ![A graphic illustrating the buying and selling of shares in a decision market](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1642543348781-ed9c6d67ed20?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

## Prediction Markets vs. Decision Markets: Key Differences

While the terms are often used interchangeably, there are crucial distinctions:

| Feature          | Prediction Market                               | Decision Market                                     |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| **Primary Goal** | Speculation and profit                               | Informed decision-making and strategic planning     |
| **Participants** | Primarily external, anonymous traders              | Internal employees, experts, and potentially customers |
| **Data Focus**   | Primarily focused on predicting future events     | Focused on actionable insights for organizational use |
* **Transparency** | Often highly transparent with public market data | Transparency can be controlled to suit organizational needs |
