## 3.  Cultivating Resilience:  Nonrigid Cognition Therapy and Training

Developing nonrigid cognition isn't a passive process; it requires conscious effort and consistent practice.  Fortunately, several methods can help you strengthen your cognitive flexibility.

**Training for Nonrigid Cognition:**

* **Cognitive Flexibility Exercises:**  These exercises, often incorporated into cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and other therapeutic approaches, involve tasks that challenge your ability to switch between different tasks or perspectives.  Examples include the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST) and various problem-solving scenarios requiring creative solutions.
* **Mindfulness Meditation:** Regular mindfulness practice helps you become more aware of your thoughts and emotions, enabling you to observe them without judgment. This increased awareness allows for greater control over your cognitive processes and reduces reactivity to unexpected events.
* **Challenging Your Biases:**  Actively seeking out diverse perspectives and challenging your own ingrained biases is crucial.  Engage in discussions with people from different backgrounds, read diverse viewpoints, and actively question your assumptions.

**Nonrigid Cognition Therapy:**

Cognitive flexibility training often forms a core component of various therapeutic approaches, particularly in treating conditions like anxiety, depression, and PTSD.  Therapists use techniques like cognitive restructuring to help individuals identify and modify rigid thinking patterns, replacing them with more adaptive and flexible approaches.
