## 2. Bridging Physics and Information Theory: A Powerful Alliance

Information theory, pioneered by Claude Shannon, provides the mathematical framework for quantifying information. It defines concepts like entropy (a measure of uncertainty or randomness) and channel capacity (the maximum rate at which information can be reliably transmitted). In physics, these concepts are finding applications in diverse areas.

Information entropy in physics, for instance, is no longer just a statistical measure of disorder. It's being explored as a fundamental property of spacetime itself. Black holes, long considered enigmatic objects, are now understood through the lens of information. The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of a black hole suggests that the information content of a black hole is proportional to its surface area, hinting at a holographic principle where the information about a volume of space is encoded on its boundary.
