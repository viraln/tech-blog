Seismic metamaterials are artificially engineered structures designed to manipulate seismic waves – the vibrations that propagate through the earth during earthquakes. Unlike traditional materials that passively absorb or reflect these waves, metamaterials actively control their propagation, leading to significant reductions in destructive energy.  They achieve this through carefully designed microstructures and geometries, creating periodic arrangements that interact with seismic waves in unique ways.  ![Microscopic view of a seismic metamaterial structure](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1581428079687-1e64df364323?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

> **Did you know?** The concept of metamaterials draws inspiration from nature.  Many natural structures, like the honeycomb of a beehive or the intricate arrangement of bones in a human hand, exhibit remarkable strength and resilience due to their optimized microstructures.

This manipulation isn't about stopping the waves entirely, which is practically impossible. Instead, it's about strategically guiding, diverting, or attenuating them to protect structures. This is achieved through phenomena like band gaps – frequency ranges where wave propagation is significantly reduced or even prohibited.
