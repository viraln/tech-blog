## What is Past-Future Arbitrage?

Past-future arbitrage, in its simplest form, involves exploiting price discrepancies between different market timelines.  This isn't about predicting the future; instead, it leverages information from past market data to identify and capitalize on inefficiencies in current market pricing.  Think of it as identifying a mispricing based on historical context and acting upon it before the market corrects itself. ##  Types of Past-Future Arbitrage Strategies

Several strategies fall under the umbrella of past-future arbitrage.  These are often tailored to specific market conditions and asset classes.

* **Statistical Arbitrage:** This involves identifying historical correlations between different assets and exploiting temporary deviations from these correlations. For instance, if two historically correlated assets diverge significantly, a statistical arbitrage strategy might involve buying the undervalued asset and shorting the overvalued one, anticipating a reversion to the mean.

* **Mean Reversion Trading:**  This strategy relies on the belief that asset prices will revert to their historical average over time. By identifying assets trading significantly below their historical mean, traders can buy with the expectation of a price increase.

* **Calendar Spread Arbitrage:** This involves exploiting price differences between contracts with different expiration dates.  For example, a trader might buy a longer-term contract and sell a shorter-term contract on the same underlying asset, profiting from the convergence of prices as the expiration dates approach.

* **Time-Series Arbitrage:** This sophisticated strategy uses complex mathematical models and historical time-series data to identify predictable patterns and anomalies in market behavior, enabling traders to anticipate price movements.
