## Understanding the Hyperobject Definition

Before we explore the revolutionary aspects, let's define "hyperobject." Coined by Timothy Morton, a hyperobject is an entity so massively distributed in time and space that it transcends localized human understanding. Think of it as something we can sense and interact with, but never fully grasp in its totality. Climate change perfectly fits this definition.

It's not just about rising temperatures or melting glaciers. It's about the interconnected web of cause and effect that stretches across continents and centuries, impacting everything from ocean currents to social structures. We can observe the effects – extreme weather events, rising sea levels – but we can't fully comprehend the hyperobject itself. This is because of the sheer scale, complexity, and non-locality of climate change.
