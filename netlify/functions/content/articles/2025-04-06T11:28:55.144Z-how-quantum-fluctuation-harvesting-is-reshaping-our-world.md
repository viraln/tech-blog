## The Casimir Effect: A Window into Zero-Point Energy

One of the most compelling pieces of evidence supporting the existence of zero-point energy is the Casimir effect. Discovered in 1948 by Dutch physicist Hendrik Casimir, this phenomenon demonstrates an attractive force between two uncharged, perfectly conducting plates placed in a vacuum.

The explanation? The plates restrict the wavelengths of virtual photons that can exist between them. Fewer virtual photons between the plates than outside results in a pressure difference, pushing the plates together. This pressure difference is a direct manifestation of zero-point energy.

While the Casimir effect produces a very small force, it's measurable and provides a tangible link to the energy inherent in the quantum vacuum. Researchers are exploring ways to amplify and control this force, potentially leading to practical energy extraction devices.

✅ **Key Point:** The Casimir effect is a cornerstone experiment demonstrating the existence and potential of zero-point energy.
