## Understanding the Simulation Hypothesis: A Deep Dive

The simulation hypothesis posits that our reality is not fundamental, but rather a simulated environment created by an advanced civilization. This concept, popularized by Nick Bostrom's 2003 paper, argues that at least one of the following propositions must be true:

1.  Humanity will almost certainly go extinct before reaching a stage where it can create realistic simulations.
2.  If humanity reaches such a stage, it will almost certainly choose not to run simulations.
3.  We are almost certainly living in a simulation.

Bostrom's trilemma doesn't prove we're in a simulation, but it highlights the logical possibilities. The argument hinges on the assumption that advanced civilizations would possess the computational power to create simulations indistinguishable from reality.
