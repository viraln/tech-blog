## The Neuroscience of Subjective Time Distortion

Our brain doesn't directly perceive time; it constructs a representation based on neural activity.  Several brain regions, including the hippocampus, cerebellum, and prefrontal cortex, play crucial roles in time perception.  Neurotransmitters like dopamine and norepinephrine also influence our subjective experience of time.  For example, heightened dopamine levels during exciting activities can compress our perception of time, making it feel shorter.
