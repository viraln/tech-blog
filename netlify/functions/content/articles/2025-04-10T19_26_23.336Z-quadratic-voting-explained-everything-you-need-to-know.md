## What is Quadratic Voting?

Quadratic voting (QV) is a novel voting mechanism designed to amplify the voices of individuals while mitigating the impact of coordinated efforts to manipulate outcomes. Unlike traditional voting systems where each person gets one vote, quadratic voting allows individuals to allocate a budget of "vote credits" across multiple options.  The crucial difference lies in the cost: the more votes you allocate to a single option, the higher the price per vote becomes.  This quadratic cost function discourages single-issue, high-intensity voting while empowering individuals to express their preferences across a wider range of issues.

> **Did you know?**  The concept of quadratic voting was initially proposed by Glen Weyl and Puja Ohlhaver in 2016, and it's rapidly evolving with new applications and research.

## How Does Quadratic Voting Work?

Imagine you have 10 vote credits. You can allocate them across different proposals:

1. **Allocate Credits:** You decide how many credits to dedicate to each proposal.  For example, you could assign 5 credits to Proposal A, 3 to Proposal B, and 2 to Proposal C.
2. **Quadratic Cost:** The cost of each vote increases quadratically.  If you allocate 'x' votes to a proposal, the cost is proportional to x².  This means allocating more votes to a single proposal becomes exponentially more expensive.
3. **Aggregate Results:** The system sums up the total weighted votes for each proposal. The proposal with the highest total wins.  The quadratic cost ensures that even a small number of voters with a strong preference for a particular proposal can significantly impact the outcome.

> **EXPERT TIP:** The specific parameters of a quadratic voting system (like the total number of vote credits per voter) can be adjusted to fine-tune the system's behavior and responsiveness to different community needs.
