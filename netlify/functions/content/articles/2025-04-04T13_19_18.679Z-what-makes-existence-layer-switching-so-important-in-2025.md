## Understanding the Core of Existence Layer Switching Explained

Existence Layer Switching (ELS) represents a paradigm shift in how we approach network security and identity management. Traditionally, network security operates at Layers 1-7 of the OSI model, focusing on data transmission, network protocols, and application-level security. ELS, however, operates *above* these layers, at what some consider Layer 8 – the "existence" layer. This layer focuses on *who* is accessing the network, *what* they are accessing, and *why*, providing a holistic view of user behavior and intent.

## The Architecture of Trust: Dissecting the ELS Architecture

Understanding the architecture of ELS is crucial to appreciating its power and potential. While specific implementations may vary, a typical ELS architecture consists of the following key components:

3.  **Trust Registry:** A distributed ledger that stores verifiable credentials and trust relationships between different entities.
4.  **Authentication and Authorization Layer:** Provides secure authentication and authorization mechanisms for accessing resources and services.
5.  **Analytics and Monitoring:** Continuously monitors user behavior and network activity to detect anomalies and potential security threats.
