## Information Theory in Physics: Applications Beyond Quantum

Information theory's influence extends far beyond quantum mechanics, impacting our understanding of:

*   **Black Hole Thermodynamics:** The Bekenstein bound suggests that the amount of information that can be contained within a region of space is limited by its surface area, not its volume. This has profound implications for understanding black holes as information processors. The holographic principle, stemming from this bound, proposes that the entire universe can be described by information encoded on a two-dimensional surface.
*   **Cosmology:** IBP offers new perspectives on the origin and evolution of the universe. Some theories suggest that the Big Bang was not a singularity but rather a phase transition in the information content of the universe.
*   **Thermodynamics:** Information is intimately linked to entropy. Reducing the entropy of a system requires information, and conversely, gaining information reduces uncertainty. Maxwell's demon, a thought experiment that seemingly violates the second law of thermodynamics, is resolved by considering the information cost of the demon's actions.
