## The Many-Worlds Interpretation: A Leading Theory

One of the most prominent interpretations of quantum mechanics supporting this idea is the Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI).  Proposed by Hugh Everett III in 1957, MWI suggests that every quantum measurement causes the universe to split into multiple branches, each representing a different possible outcome.  In this view, every decision, every quantum event, leads to a branching of reality, creating a constantly expanding multiverse.

> **EXPERT TIP:**  Understanding the Many-Worlds Interpretation requires grappling with the concept of decoherence – the process by which a quantum system loses its coherence and behaves classically.  This is a key area of ongoing research.

## Universe Superposition and the Double-Slit Experiment

The classic double-slit experiment provides a compelling illustration of superposition at a quantum level.  When electrons are fired at a screen with two slits, they create an interference pattern, suggesting they passed through both slits simultaneously. This demonstrates superposition – the electron exists in multiple states (passing through both slits) until measured.  Extending this principle to the universe suggests our reality might be similarly indeterminate until observed.
