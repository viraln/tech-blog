## The Neuroscience Behind Time Perception

While the exact neural mechanisms underlying time perception are still being unraveled, researchers have identified key brain regions involved in the process. The prefrontal cortex, responsible for executive functions and working memory, plays a crucial role in estimating durations. The cerebellum, associated with motor control and coordination, is also involved in timing sensory and motor events. The basal ganglia, a group of structures deep within the brain, are thought to be involved in procedural learning and the timing of sequential actions.

⚡ **Key Brain Regions Involved in Time Perception:**
*   Prefrontal Cortex: Executive functions, working memory, duration estimation.
*   Cerebellum: Timing sensory and motor events, coordination.
*   Basal Ganglia: Procedural learning, timing sequential actions.

Neurotransmitters like dopamine and norepinephrine also play a significant role. Dopamine, associated with reward and motivation, tends to speed up our perception of time, while norepinephrine, linked to stress and arousal, can slow it down. Understanding these neurochemical influences opens avenues for biohacking our time perception.
