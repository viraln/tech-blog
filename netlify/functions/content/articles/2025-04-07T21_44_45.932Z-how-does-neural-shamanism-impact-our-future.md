## The Neuroscience of Shamanic Practices: Unveiling the Brain's Secrets

One of the key areas of investigation in neural shamanism is the study of altered states of consciousness (ASCs). Shamanic practices, such as drumming, chanting, meditation, and the use of plant medicines, are known to induce ASCs, which are characterized by shifts in perception, cognition, and sense of self.

Neuroscientists use various tools, such as electroencephalography (EEG), functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and magnetoencephalography (MEG), to study brain activity during these states. These studies have revealed that ASCs are associated with specific changes in brainwave patterns, neurotransmitter levels, and neural network connectivity.

For example:

*   **EEG studies** have shown that shamanic drumming can induce alpha and theta brainwave activity, which are associated with relaxation, meditation, and altered states of consciousness.
*   **fMRI studies** have revealed that shamanic journeying can activate areas of the brain involved in imagery, emotion, and self-awareness, such as the default mode network (DMN) and the limbic system.
*   Research into **plant medicines** like ayahuasca has shown that they can modulate serotonin and dopamine levels in the brain, leading to profound changes in perception and consciousness.

**Did you know?** The DMN, often referred to as the "seat of the self," is typically suppressed during focused attention tasks. However, in shamanic states, the DMN can become more active, leading to a heightened sense of interconnectedness and spiritual experience.

This research is helping us understand how shamanic practices can affect the brain and potentially promote healing, personal growth, and spiritual development.
