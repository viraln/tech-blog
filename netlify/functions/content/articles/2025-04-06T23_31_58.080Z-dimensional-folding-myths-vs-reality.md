## What is Dimensional Folding (And What It Isn't)

Dimensional folding, often referred to as space folding, is a theoretical concept that involves manipulating the fabric of spacetime to bring two distant points closer together. Imagine folding a piece of paper so that two points on opposite ends touch; dimensional folding aims to achieve something similar with space itself. This differs drastically from simply traveling *through* space at high speeds. It's about altering the *distance* between two points.

It's crucial to distinguish dimensional folding from other faster-than-light (FTL) travel concepts, such as wormholes. While both aim to bypass the limitations of the speed of light, they operate on different principles. Wormholes are hypothetical tunnels connecting two distant points in spacetime, whereas dimensional folding aims to directly manipulate the space between those points. ![A visual comparison of wormholes and dimensional folding, showing the difference in spatial manipulation.](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1597566833495-e4ad471783ec?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
