## What is Star Lifting: A Definition

Star lifting, also known as stellar lifting, is a hypothetical megastructure engineering concept that involves extracting matter from a star, typically plasma from the star's outer layers. This extracted material could then be used for various purposes, such as fueling fusion reactors, building megastructures, or even extending the star's lifespan. ## How Does Star Lifting Work? The Proposed Technologies

The core challenge of star lifting lies in overcoming the immense gravitational forces and extreme temperatures present on the surface of a star. Several theoretical methods have been proposed, each relying on different physical principles:

*   **Electromagnetic Methods:** These techniques involve using powerful magnetic fields to capture ionized plasma from the star's corona or photosphere. The magnetic fields would then channel the plasma towards a collection point.
*   **Mechanical Methods:** This approach envisions using physical structures, such as rotating tethers or scoops, to "skim" material from the star's surface. This is arguably the most challenging approach due to the extreme conditions.
*   **Laser Ablation:** This involves using powerful lasers to vaporize material from the star's surface, creating a stream of plasma that can be collected.
*   **Mass Driver Assisted Lifting:** This method proposes using a mass driver system built around the star. Material scooped from the surface would be accelerated and launched into space, where it could be collected.

Each of these methods has its own advantages and disadvantages. Electromagnetic methods are generally considered the most promising due to their potential for high efficiency and relatively low risk to the star's stability. Mechanical methods, while conceptually simple, face immense engineering hurdles due to the extreme temperatures and gravitational forces.

> **EXPERT TIP:** The efficiency of star lifting is paramount. A highly inefficient method could destabilize the star or consume more energy than it extracts.
