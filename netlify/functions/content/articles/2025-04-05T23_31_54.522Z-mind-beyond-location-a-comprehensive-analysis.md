## 2. Extended Mind Theory: Where Does Your Mind Begin and End?

Before diving into psychic abilities, let's consider a more grounded concept: extended mind theory. Proposed by philosophers Andy Clark and David Chalmers, this theory suggests that the mind doesn't stop at the skull. Instead, it extends to include external objects and tools that we routinely use to augment our cognitive abilities.

For example, consider someone with Alzheimer's disease who relies on a notebook to remember appointments and navigate their daily life. According to extended mind theory, the notebook isn't just a tool; it's an integral part of that person's cognitive system. Similarly, our smartphones, computers, and even the internet can be seen as extensions of our minds, allowing us to access information, solve problems, and communicate with others in ways that wouldn't be possible otherwise.

This theory provides a valuable framework for understanding how our minds are intertwined with the world around us, blurring the lines between internal and external cognition. It also paves the way for exploring the possibility that our minds could potentially extend even further, beyond the physical realm.

Remote viewing (RV) is a controversial but fascinating technique that claims to allow individuals to perceive information about distant locations or events, even without using their physical senses. Typically, a "viewer" is given coordinates or a target description and asked to describe what they "see" or "experience" at that location. ![Example of a remote viewing session with a viewer describing a target location](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1667679692108-6f0a1b950299?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

During the Cold War, the US government conducted research into RV, hoping to gain a strategic advantage by gathering intelligence remotely. The Stargate Project, as it was known, lasted for over two decades. While the program was eventually shut down, the results remain a subject of debate. Some argue that the RV data was accurate enough to be useful, while others dismiss it as anecdotal or explainable by chance and suggestion.

Regardless of the debate, RV continues to be practiced and studied by researchers and enthusiasts around the world. Proponents suggest that it demonstrates the ability of consciousness to transcend space and time, while skeptics argue that the results are often vague, subjective, and prone to interpretation bias.

**Did you know?** The term "remote viewing" was coined by physicists Harold Puthoff and Russell Targ at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI) during their research into psychic abilities in the 1970s.

Telepathy, the direct transfer of thoughts or feelings from one person to another without the use of sensory channels, is another aspect of non-local consciousness that has fascinated humanity for centuries.
