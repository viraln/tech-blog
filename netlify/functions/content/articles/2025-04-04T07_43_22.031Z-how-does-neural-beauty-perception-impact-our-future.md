## Facial Attractiveness: Neural Correlates and Evolutionary Significance

Facial attractiveness is a particularly well-studied area within neural beauty perception. Research has consistently shown that certain facial features are universally considered attractive. These include:

*   **Symmetry:** Symmetrical faces are generally perceived as more attractive, possibly because symmetry is a marker of developmental stability and genetic health.
*   **Averageness:** Faces that are mathematically "average" (created by morphing multiple faces together) are often rated as more attractive than individual faces. This may be because average faces are less likely to carry unusual or potentially harmful genetic mutations.
*   **Sexual Dimorphism:** Features that accentuate the differences between male and female faces (e.g., a strong jawline in men, full lips in women) are often considered attractive, signaling reproductive fitness.

Neuroimaging studies have shown that viewing attractive faces activates the same reward centers in the brain as viewing beautiful art or listening to pleasing music. This suggests that facial attractiveness is processed as a fundamentally rewarding stimulus.

⚡ **Did you know?:** Studies have shown that even infants, who have not yet been exposed to cultural beauty standards, show a preference for attractive faces. This supports the idea that some aspects of facial attractiveness are innate.
