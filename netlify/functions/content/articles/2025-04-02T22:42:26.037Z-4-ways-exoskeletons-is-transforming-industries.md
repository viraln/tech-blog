### The Power of Assistance: How Industrial Exoskeletons Work

**How do exoskeletons work** in industrial settings? They typically consist of a frame worn on the body, often made of lightweight materials like carbon fiber or aluminum. Sensors and actuators work in tandem to detect the user's movements and provide assistance at key joints, such as the back, shoulders, and legs. **Powered exoskeletons** use motors to amplify the user's strength, while passive exoskeletons rely on springs and dampers to redistribute weight and reduce strain.

⚡**Key Benefits of Industrial Exoskeletons:**

*   ✅ Reduced risk of musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs)
*   ✅ Increased productivity and efficiency
*   ✅ Extended worker lifespan and reduced absenteeism
*   ✅ Improved worker comfort and morale
*   ✅ Enhanced quality of work due to reduced fatigue

### Restoring Movement and Independence: The Mechanics of Medical Exoskeletons

Medical exoskeletons function by using sensors to detect the user's intended movements and then employing motors and actuators to move the legs and body accordingly. Some devices are controlled by the user's own muscle activity (using electromyography or EMG), while others are pre-programmed or controlled by a therapist. The goal is to provide the necessary support and assistance to enable users to perform activities that would otherwise be impossible.

🔑 **Key Applications of Medical Exoskeletons:**

*   🔑 Rehabilitation after stroke or spinal cord injury
*   🔑 Assisting individuals with mobility impairments to walk
*   🔑 Improving balance and coordination
*   🔑 Reducing secondary complications associated with immobility (e.g., pressure sores, muscle atrophy)
*   🔑 Enhancing quality of life and independence

In logistics, exoskeletons primarily focus on providing back support during lifting tasks.
