## Factors Affecting Diffusion: A Deeper Dive

Understanding the factors that influence diffusion is crucial for optimizing its application in various fields.

*   **Temperature:** Higher temperatures generally lead to faster diffusion rates as molecules possess more kinetic energy.
*   **Concentration Gradient:** A steeper concentration gradient results in a higher diffusion rate.
*   **Size and Mass of the Diffusing Particles:** Smaller and lighter particles diffuse faster than larger and heavier ones.
*   **Medium:** Diffusion is faster in gases than in liquids, and slowest in solids due to the varying degrees of freedom of movement.
*   **Pressure:** Pressure can affect diffusion, particularly in gases.

> **EXPERT TIP:** When working with diffusion-based systems, carefully control temperature, pressure, and concentration gradients to achieve the desired results. Accurate monitoring and control are essential for reproducibility and optimization.

## Diffusion vs. Osmosis: Clarifying the Key Difference

While often confused, diffusion and osmosis are distinct processes. Diffusion is the movement of any substance from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration. Osmosis, on the other hand, is the movement of *water* across a semi-permeable membrane from an area of high water concentration (low solute concentration) to an area of low water concentration (high solute concentration). Osmosis is essentially a specific type of diffusion.

| Feature          | Diffusion                               | Osmosis                                     |
|-------------------|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Substance Moving | Any substance (solute or solvent)        | Water (solvent)                             |
| Membrane         | Not necessarily required                | Semi-permeable membrane required            |
| Driving Force    | Concentration gradient                  | Water potential gradient (osmotic pressure) |
