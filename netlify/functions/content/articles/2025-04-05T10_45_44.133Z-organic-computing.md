Organic Computing draws inspiration from biological systems, mimicking their ability to self-organize, adapt, and evolve. Unlike traditional computing, which relies on rigid programming and predefined rules, OC aims to create systems that are more robust, flexible, and resilient. Central to OC are the following key principles:

*   **Self-Organization:** Systems can structure and restructure themselves without explicit external commands. This allows them to adapt to unexpected changes and optimize their performance.
*   **Self-Configuration:** Components can automatically configure themselves to work together, reducing the need for manual configuration and minimizing errors.
*   **Self-Optimization:** Systems continuously monitor their performance and adjust their parameters to achieve optimal efficiency and effectiveness.
*   **Self-Healing:** Systems can detect and recover from errors and failures automatically, minimizing downtime and ensuring continuous operation.
*   **Self-Protection:** Systems can proactively defend themselves against threats and attacks, enhancing security and reliability.
*   **Context Awareness:** Systems can perceive and respond to their environment, adapting their behavior to specific situations.

These principles are not just theoretical concepts. They are the building blocks of a new generation of IT systems that are more intelligent, autonomous, and user-friendly.
