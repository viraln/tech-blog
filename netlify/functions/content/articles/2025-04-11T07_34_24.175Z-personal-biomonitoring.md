Personal biomonitoring, also known as personal exposure monitoring, measures the levels of specific substances in a person's body. This differs from environmental biomonitoring, which focuses on assessing pollutants in the environment itself.  Instead, personal biomonitoring provides a direct assessment of an individual's internal exposure, offering a more personalized and accurate picture of their health risks.  The substances measured can range from heavy metals like lead and mercury to pesticides, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and even certain pharmaceuticals. ## Biomonitoring Methods: How it's Done

Several methods are used for personal biomonitoring, each with its strengths and limitations:

* **Biological Samples:** This is the most common approach.  Samples such as blood, urine, hair, nails, and even breath can be analyzed for specific contaminants.  The choice of sample depends on the substance being measured and the duration of exposure.
    * 🔑 **Blood tests:** Provide a snapshot of current exposure levels.
    * ⚡ **Urine tests:** Reflect exposure over a shorter period, typically the last 24 hours.
    * ✅ **Hair and nail analysis:** Offer a longer-term picture of exposure, potentially spanning months or even years.

* **Passive Samplers:** These devices collect environmental pollutants over a specific time period, giving an indication of the individual's exposure to airborne contaminants.  They are often worn like a badge and require less invasive sampling. ![Example of a passive sampler being worn.](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1514904215020-2f15586b250e?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)
