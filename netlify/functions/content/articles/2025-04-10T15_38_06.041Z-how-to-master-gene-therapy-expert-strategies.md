## What is Gene Therapy? A Comprehensive Overview

Gene therapy fundamentally involves modifying a person's genes to treat or prevent disease.  Unlike traditional treatments that manage symptoms, gene therapy aims to correct the underlying genetic defect.  This can be achieved through several methods, including adding a functional gene to compensate for a faulty one, silencing a harmful gene, or correcting a gene's mutation. ![Microscopic image of gene editing process](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1578496480240-32d3e0c04525?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

There are two main approaches:  *in vivo* therapy, where the gene is delivered directly into the patient's body, and *ex vivo* therapy, where cells are removed, modified in a lab, and then reintroduced.  The choice depends on the disease and the target cells.

## CAR T-Cell Therapy: A Promising Cancer Treatment

CAR T-cell therapy is a type of gene therapy that involves modifying a patient's T cells (a type of immune cell) to target and destroy cancer cells.  This approach has shown remarkable success in treating certain types of leukemia and lymphoma.  However, it also carries potential side effects, including cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity.  Research is focused on improving the safety and effectiveness of CAR T-cell therapy.
