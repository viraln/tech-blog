## What is Xenotechnology? A New Paradigm in Engineering

Xenotechnology, at its core, is the creation and utilization of biological machines built from living cells, specifically cells not typically used for locomotion or robotics. The term "xeno" refers to the use of biological material from an organism different from what's traditionally used in robotics, or even from what's found in the target environment. The most prominent example of this is the xenobot, a programmable organism assembled from frog ( *Xenopus laevis*, hence the name) embryonic stem cells.

Unlike traditional robots made of metal and plastic, xenobots are entirely biological. They are designed using algorithms and then assembled by robotic systems or, in some cases, they self-assemble. This unique approach allows for the creation of machines with unprecedented capabilities, including self-healing, biodegradability, and the ability to function in environments hostile to conventional robots.

The algorithm tested millions of potential configurations, selecting those that performed best in the simulated environment.
