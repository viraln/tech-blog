## What are Entangled Community Networks?

Entangled Community Networks are decentralized, community-owned and operated internet infrastructures. Unlike traditional internet service providers (ISPs), ECNs empower local communities to build, manage, and control their own network resources. These networks often utilize a combination of technologies, including Wi-Fi mesh networks, fiber optics, and even innovative technologies like TV White Space, to provide affordable and reliable internet access. The "entangled" aspect refers to the interconnectedness and collaboration fostered between community members and organizations in building and maintaining the network.

## 1. Revolutionizing Access: Bridging the Digital Divide

The most obvious, yet profoundly impactful, aspect of ECNs is their ability to bridge the digital divide. Traditional ISPs often neglect underserved areas due to low profitability, leaving rural communities and low-income neighborhoods without adequate internet access. ECNs address this problem head-on by:

*   🔑 **Prioritizing Community Needs:** ECNs are driven by the needs of the community, not profit margins. This means that underserved areas are more likely to receive attention and resources.
*   ⚡ **Affordable Access:** By eliminating the overhead and profit motives of large corporations, ECNs can offer internet access at significantly lower prices, making it accessible to a wider range of people. Many ECNs offer tiered pricing or even free basic access to ensure inclusivity.
*   ✅ **Local Ownership:** Local ownership ensures that the network remains accountable to the community and that its services are tailored to meet local needs. This fosters digital equity and empowers residents to participate fully in the digital economy.

> **EXPERT TIP:** Successful ECNs often partner with local organizations, libraries, and schools to provide digital literacy training and support, ensuring that community members can effectively utilize the network.

**Did you know?** In 2025, over 40% of rural households in the US still lack access to broadband internet, highlighting the urgent need for alternative solutions like ECNs.

**Example:** The Detroit Community Technology Project (DCTP) in Detroit, Michigan, is a prime example of an ECN addressing the digital divide. DCTP builds and supports community-owned wireless networks in underserved neighborhoods, providing affordable internet access and digital literacy training to residents. They’ve seen marked improvements in residents’ ability to access educational resources, job opportunities, and essential services.

## 2. Revolutionizing Control: Decentralization and Data Sovereignty

Beyond simply providing access, ECNs revolutionize control over the internet. Traditional internet models concentrate power in the hands of a few large corporations, raising concerns about data privacy, censorship, and net neutrality. ECNs offer a powerful alternative:

*   🛡️ **Data Sovereignty:** ECNs allow communities to control their own data. This means that data generated within the network remains within the community, reducing the risk of surveillance and exploitation by outside entities.
*   🌐 **Decentralized Infrastructure:** By distributing network infrastructure across multiple locations, ECNs become more resilient to outages and censorship. A single point of failure cannot bring down the entire network.
*   ✍️ **Community Governance:** ECNs typically operate under a community governance model, where residents have a say in how the network is managed and operated. This fosters transparency and accountability, ensuring that the network serves the best interests of the community.
