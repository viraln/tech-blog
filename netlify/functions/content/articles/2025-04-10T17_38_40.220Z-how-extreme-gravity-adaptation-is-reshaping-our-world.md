## Understanding the Effects of Extreme Gravity

Extreme gravity, or high-g, refers to gravitational forces significantly exceeding Earth's 1g.  These forces can have profound effects on the human body, primarily due to the redistribution of blood and other fluids.  At high-g, blood pools in the lower extremities, leading to reduced blood flow to the brain, causing a phenomenon known as "g-LOC" (g-induced loss of consciousness).  Other effects include blurred vision, tunnel vision, and extreme physical discomfort. ![Illustration showing blood pooling in the legs under high-g](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1572417051886-c16f1253a604?q=85&w=1200&fit=max&fm=webp&auto=compress)

> **Did you know?**  Even relatively low levels of sustained high-g can cause significant physiological stress, highlighting the need for effective adaptation strategies.

The severity of these effects depends on the magnitude and duration of the g-force, the direction of the force (positive, negative, or lateral), and individual factors like physical fitness and training.  Understanding these factors is crucial for developing effective adaptation techniques.
