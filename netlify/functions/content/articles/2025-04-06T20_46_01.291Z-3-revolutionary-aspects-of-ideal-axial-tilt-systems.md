## 1. The Goldilocks Zone of Axial Tilt: Finding the Sweet Spot

The concept of a habitable zone around a star is well-known, but equally important is the "Goldilocks zone" of axial tilt. This refers to the range of axial tilt angles that are most conducive to stable climates and the development of life.

*   **Too Little Tilt:** A planet with little to no axial tilt (close to 0°) would experience minimal seasonal variations. While this might seem ideal, the lack of seasonal mixing can lead to extreme temperature differences between the equator and the poles. The poles could become perpetually frozen, while the equator could become unbearably hot, limiting the habitable area.

*   **Too Much Tilt:** Conversely, a planet with a very high axial tilt (approaching or exceeding 90°) would experience extreme and unpredictable seasons. Regions near the equator could experience prolonged periods of darkness and sunlight, leading to dramatic temperature swings and making it difficult for life to adapt.
