## Key Components of Spatial Grammar

Understanding spatial grammar requires familiarity with its core components:

*   **Reference Objects:** These are the objects or locations that serve as the point of reference for describing the location of other objects. For example, in the phrase "The book is *on* the table," the table is the reference object.
*   **Figure Objects:** These are the objects whose location is being described in relation to the reference object. In the same example, "the book" is the figure object.
*   **Spatial Prepositions:** These are words like "on," "under," "next to," "in front of," "behind," "between," and "through" that specify the spatial relationship between the figure and reference objects.
*   **Frames of Reference:** These are systems for defining location and direction. Common frames of reference include:
    *   **Intrinsic (Object-Centered):** Describing location relative to the object itself (e.g., "the front of the car").
    *   **Relative (Viewer-Centered):** Describing location relative to the speaker's perspective (e.g., "the tree is to my left").
    *   **Absolute (Environment-Centered):** Describing location relative to fixed coordinates like cardinal directions (e.g., "the house is north of the lake").
*   **Perspective:** This refers to the viewpoint from which the spatial relationship is being described. The chosen perspective can significantly alter the description.

⚡ **Key Point:** The choice of frame of reference heavily influences how spatial information is encoded and understood.
